## 1. Current status of Polymarket US (launch, market categories, states, KYC, funding, API)

### Takeaway
Polymarket US is live for US residents as a CFTC-regulated designated contract market (QCX LLC) with an official Retail API and SDKs, and by late 2026 offers sports, politics/elections, economics, crypto and culture contracts, not only sports. State availability is contested and sources conflict sharply on Illinois (listed as excluded by several guides, listed as available by the most recent one); no source found lists Iowa as excluded.

## 2. Is international polymarket.com still geoblocked for US persons? ToS on US persons and VPNs; enforcement

### Takeaway
Yes. The international site still excludes US persons and routes Americans to Polymarket US; its Terms of Use prohibit using a VPN or similar tool to get around geographic restrictions, and the CFTC reopened a probe (reported October 27, 2026) into whether Polymarket has done enough to keep US users off the offshore platform. For a US resident, trading on polymarket.com is a ToS violation with account/fund risk and rising regulatory attention; it is not a legitimate access route.

### Cited Findings
- 2022 baseline: the CFTC found Polymarket's operating entity offered event contracts to US persons without registration; the settlement required winding down non-compliant markets, blocking US access, and a $1.4 million civil penalty [news] — [CNBC, Oct 27, 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/cftc-probes-polymarket-over-2022-settlement.html); [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/is-polymarket-legal-in-the-usa/)
- polymarket.com blocks US trading and points US persons to polymarket.us [3p] — [predscope.com](https://predscope.com/guide/how-to-use-polymarket-in-us); [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/prediction-markets/is-polymarket-legal-in-the-usa/)
- The Terms of Use bar VPNs and similar tools used to circumvent geographic restrictions; third parties cite Section 2.1.4 and Polymarket's geographic-restrictions page, and say Polymarket states it detects such use [3p descriptions of the ToS, not the ToS text] — [tradetheoutcome.com on Section 2.1.4](https://www.tradetheoutcome.com/polymarket-section-2-1-4/); [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/editorial/technology/can-you-use-a-vpn-on-polymarket-what-the-rules-allow-and-what-locks-your-account/)
- Polymarket's terms also reportedly prohibit wash trading, spoofing, front-running, orders that do not change beneficial ownership, manipulative trading, and VPN use to get around location limits [3p/news] — [BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/polymarket-kalshi-insider-trading-rules/)
- Reported consequences: accounts caught circumventing restrictions can be frozen with funds locked; one outlet says that since mid-2026 the platform blocks known VPN address ranges, suspends suspicious accounts without notice and pushes active traders into ID checks (single source, hedged with "reportedly"; start date given variously as May or June 2026) [3p] — [cryptonews.net](https://cryptonews.net/editorial/technology/can-you-use-a-vpn-on-polymarket-what-the-rules-allow-and-what-locks-your-account/); [polymarket101.com](https://www.polymarket101.com/en/docs/faq/vpn-and-geo-restrictions/)
- "Close-only" status: one source lists the US among close-only jurisdictions on the international site (existing positions can be closed, no new ones opened); another describes a plain block. Unresolved [3p] — [librepass.co](https://librepass.co/learn/polymarket-geoblock/); [airdropalert.com](https://airdropalert.com/blogs/polymarket-restricted-countries/)
- CFTC probe (reported October 27, 2026, Reuters-sourced): the agency is again examining whether Polymarket broke the 2022 settlement, including whether it did enough to keep US users off the offshore platform, how it verifies user location, and what it advertises in the US. The earlier inquiry was closed in 2025 without charges and has been reopened; it sits within a broader 2026 CFTC investigation that followed a Wall Street Journal report on simulated-trade marketing videos [news] — [US News/Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-10-27/exclusive-us-agency-is-again-probing-if-polymarket-broke-2022-settlement-sources-says); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/cftc-probes-polymarket-over-2022-settlement.html); [Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/28/cftc-reopens-polymarket-probe-over-possible-breach-of-us-user-ban-deal/)
- The CNBC report says the review also covers onboarding and customer protections on Polymarket's US platform [news] — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/27/cftc-probes-polymarket-over-2022-settlement.html)
- Criminal exposure example: a US soldier was charged in April 2026; the indictment says he accessed the platform via a VPN geolocating to a foreign country; he has pleaded not guilty (the charged conduct is reported in the context of the probe coverage; details of the underlying charges were not retrieved) [news] — [US News/Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-10-27/exclusive-us-agency-is-again-probing-if-polymarket-broke-2022-settlement-sources-says)
- Company position: Polymarket says it revamped controls; its global head of investigations said in August 2026 that US users circumventing restrictions was not "a really prevalent issue" [news] — [US News/Reuters](https://www.usnews.com/news/top-news/articles/2026-10-27/exclusive-us-agency-is-again-probing-if-polymarket-broke-2022-settlement-sources-says)

### Inferences
- With a reopened CFTC probe focused specifically on US-user leakage, the direction of travel in late 2026 is toward stricter location and identity checks on the international site, which raises the probability of frozen or close-only accounts for any US person there. ## 3. State-level actions 2025-2026 (Illinois and Iowa in particular) and the federal CFTC posture

### Takeaway
The CFTC under Chairman Selig is strongly pro-prediction-market and is suing states (including Illinois) to assert exclusive federal jurisdiction, but the courts are split: the Third Circuit sided with Kalshi (April 2026) while the Ninth (August 2026) and Sixth (September 25, 2026) Circuits sided with the states on sports contracts, and Supreme Court review is being sought. Illinois is an active battleground (Gaming Board cease-and-desists, a federal suit against the state, a Kalshi suit); in Iowa, Kalshi sued pre-emptively in March 2026 and was reportedly denied a preliminary injunction. The fights centre on sports contracts.

### Inferences
- The state fights are overwhelmingly about sports contracts. Non-sports categories (economics, politics, crypto, weather) have drawn far less state enforcement, though Iowa's dispute explicitly includes election-wagering provisions. A bot limited to non-sports markets carries lower state-law disruption risk than a sports bot.
- Illinois and Iowa sit in the Seventh and Eighth Circuits respectively; neither circuit has ruled in the sources found, so access in both states depends on platform risk decisions and district-court outcomes. Practical risk for the user is a sudden state restriction or category removal (as with Michigan sports contracts on Polymarket US ending November 25, 2026), which would more plausibly force position close-out than loss of funds on a regulated venue.
- The circuit split makes Supreme Court review plausible, but no cert grant was found as of the latest sources (early October 2026).

## 4. Full fee picture on both Polymarket venues (trading fees, deposits/withdrawals, gas, rewards)

### Takeaway
Neither Polymarket venue is free any more. International: taker-only fee of shares x rate x p x (1-p) with category rates of 0.07 (crypto), 0.05 (sports), 0.04 (politics/finance/tech), 0 (geopolitics), makers pay nothing; a new schedule takes effect at 23:59 UTC on November 20, 2026. Polymarket US: volume-tiered taker coefficient from 0.095 (under $10,000 of 30-day volume) down to 0.0695, which at the entry tier is about 36% above Kalshi's 0.07; makers pay nothing on standard markets.

### Cited Findings
**Polymarket International (polymarket.com)**
- Official formula: fee = C x feeRate x p x (1 - p), C = shares traded, p = share price; no exponent term in the official formula [official, via search summary] — [docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)
- Official taker rates by category: Crypto 0.07; Sports 0.05; Finance, Politics, Tech 0.04; Geopolitics 0; Economics, Culture, Weather, Mentions, Other/General listed with category-specific rates; Combos 0.05 with 0% rebate. ## 5. Kalshi comparison (fees, API, coverage, minimum sizes, interest)

### Takeaway
Kalshi is the most established regulated alternative: taker fee roundup(0.07 x C x P x (1-P)), maker fee zero on most series but 0.0175 on a growing list (160 series as of November 11, 2026), a free retail API with tiered rate limits, interest of roughly 3.5% to 4% on balances and open positions (minimum $250 average balance), and availability in all 50 states with sports contracts blocked in a handful.

### Cited Findings
- Fee formula: fee = roundup(0.07 x C x P x (1 - P)), C = contracts, P = price in dollars; maximum $1.75 per 100 contracts at 50 cents [official PDF via search summary; 3p] — [Kalshi fee schedule PDF, "Fee Schedule for July 2026 - 7.7.26 Update"](https://kalshi.com/docs/kalshi-fee-schedule.pdf); [quantvps.com](https://www.quantvps.com/blog/kalshi-fees-explained)

- Maker fees: resting orders pay nothing unless the series charges maker fees, in which case the coefficient is 0.0175 instead of 0.07; as of November 11, 2026, 160 Kalshi series carried maker fees, 107 of them sports [3p] — [rivermarkets.com Kalshi fees](https://www.rivermarkets.com/insights/kalshi-fees.html); [totalis.trade Kalshi fees](https://www.totalis.trade/blogs/prediction-markets/kalshi-fees)

### Inferences

## 6. Explicit rules about bots / automated trading / AI agents

### Takeaway
Automated trading on one's own account is an expected, supported use on Kalshi, Polymarket US and the international Polymarket CLOB; no source found any rule that singles out AI agents. The binding constraints are rate limits, the prohibition on manipulative practices (wash trading, spoofing, self-matching), and on Kalshi a Developer Agreement that limits API use to your own trading.

### Cited Findings
- Kalshi publishes an API and bots trading a member's own account are an expected use; restrictions come from the Developer Agreement: API use is limited to facilitating your own trading, Kalshi can cap API calls without notice, facilitating trading for other members and sublicensing the API are barred, and sharing API data requires written authorization; spoofing and wash sales are named as prohibited [3p summary of official agreement] — [traadence.com](https://www.traadence.com/blog/kalshi-automated-trading-bot-rules)

- Polymarket International: official docs walk traders through placing orders and running a market-making system from their own account. Official changelog lists CLOB limits of 120,000 POST /order requests per 10 minutes (200 per second sustained) and a 2,000 per 10 seconds burst for batch endpoints; third-party guides cite a general CLOB limit of 9,000 requests per 10 seconds and 1,500 per 10 seconds on /book and /price, plus per-signer order/cancel buckets from July 2026 [official changelog via search summary; 3p] — [docs.polymarket.com changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/changelog); [polytest.io](https://www.polytest.io/blog/polymarket-api-rate-limits); [agentbets.ai rate limits](https://agentbets.ai/guides/polymarket-rate-limits-guide/)

### Inferences
- A single-account personal bot that trades its owner's money is within the rules on all three venues. Design implications: never cross your own orders (self-match prevention), do not place orders you intend to cancel to move the price, do not run multiple accounts, and do not resell or republish API data.
- For a small bot, rate limits are not a practical constraint on any venue (10+ order writes per second on Kalshi Basic, a reported 20 requests per second on Polymarket US).
- The Polymarket data-use clause targets professional firms; an individual retail trader is not obviously covered, but the exact definition should be read in the terms.
- An LLM-driven agent has no special status in the rules found; the account holder remains fully responsible for what it does.

## 7. US tax treatment of prediction-market gains and losses

### Takeaway
Unsettled. The IRS has issued no formal guidance on classifying event-contract income (as of mid to late 2026), so gambling income, capital gain/loss, and Section 1256 treatment are all argued; neither Kalshi nor Polymarket issues a comprehensive 1099 for trading profit. The 2026 OBBBA change capping gambling-loss deductions at 90% of losses applies only if the activity is treated as gambling, and in that case can create tax on a break-even year.

### Cited Findings
- No IRS guidance: the IRS had not issued formal prediction-market guidance as of July 16, 2026, and one guide says none as of October 2026 [3p CPA/tax blogs] — [monacocpa.cpa](https://www.monacocpa.cpa/prediction-market-tax); [predictionhunt.com](https://www.predictionhunt.com/blog/prediction-market-taxes-2026-polymarket-kalshi-irs)
- All winnings are taxable regardless of whether a form is issued [3p consensus] — [coinledger.io](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax)
- Competing classifications: (a) gambling income, described by one guide as the most conservative position for sports-outcome contracts; (b) capital gain/loss on a financial contract; (c) Section 1256 60/40 treatment, described as plausible but unsettled because the CFTC generally classifies these contracts as binary-option swaps and DCM listing alone does not confer Section 1256 status. One guide asserts flatly that Kalshi contracts qualify under Section 1256; others dispute it [3p; academic commentary also exists] — [coselite.com on Section 1256](https://coselite.com/blog/section-1256-event-contracts); [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/news/taking-a-gamble-on-prediction-market-taxation); [TaxProf Blog, Jan 17, 2026](https://taxprofblog.aals.org/2026/01/17/sports-gambling-and-the-taxation-of-prediction-markets); [Keeper](https://wf.keepertax.com/posts/how-to-file-taxes-on-kalshi-and-polymarket)
- OBBBA 90% cap: starting with tax year 2026, gambling-loss deductions are limited to 90% of losses and cannot exceed winnings; losses are deductible only if you itemize on Schedule A; under the cap a break-even gambler owes tax [3p tax guides/news] — [Keeper](https://www.keepertax.com/posts/are-gambling-losses-tax-deductible); [defirate](https://defirate.com/news/gambling-tax-deduction-cap-migration-prediction-markets/); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/?p=175712)
- Whether the cap reaches prediction markets depends on classification: one author argues Kalshi users are shielded because their losses are capital losses; this is a position, not settled law [3p] — [defirate](https://defirate.com/news/gambling-tax-deduction-cap-migration-prediction-markets/)
- H.R. 10357 would restore the full gambling-loss deduction from tax year 2026; status unverified, treat as not enacted [3p] — [beancount.io](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/07/prediction-market-winnings-tax-kalshi-polymarket-guide)
- Kalshi reporting: its tax documentation lists Forms 1099-INT, 1099-MISC, 1099-B and 1099-DA for specific activities (interest, rewards, crypto transfers) but no 1099 that reports event-contract trading profit; users get a profit and loss statement. One guide claims Kalshi issues 1099-B for settlements; most contradict it [3p, conflicting] — [coselite.com Kalshi taxes](https://coselite.com/blog/kalshi-taxes); [awaken.tax Kalshi](https://awaken.tax/media/article/kalshi-taxes); [contracttax.com](https://www.contracttax.com/guides/kalshi-taxes)
- Polymarket: sources say it issues no tax forms; redeeming or selling outcome tokens is the taxable event even if USDC stays in the wallet (this describes the international crypto product) [3p] — [coinledger.io](https://coinledger.io/blog/prediction-markets-tax); [awaken.tax](https://awaken.tax/media/article/prediction-market-taxes)
- Robinhood: sources conflict on whether it issues 1099-B for event contracts [3p] — [oddsreference.com](https://oddsreference.com/predictions/learn/prediction-market-taxes); [awaken.tax](https://awaken.tax/media/article/prediction-market-taxes)
- Practitioner uncertainty is real: even a tax expert who won on Kalshi was reported as still working out how to file [news] — [AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/news/even-tax-expert-won-big-103901903.html)

### Inferences
- The bot should keep a complete, timestamped trade ledger (fills, fees, settlements, rewards, interest) because no venue supplies a comprehensive tax form and the taxpayer must be able to support whichever classification a tax adviser chooses.
- Classification drives outcomes materially for a high-turnover bot: under gambling treatment gross wins are income and losses are an itemized deduction capped at 90% (bad for an account that takes the standard deduction and for any near-break-even strategy); under capital treatment gains and losses net, with a $3,000 annual limit on net losses against ordinary income (that limit is general tax background, not from the sources above).
- Illinois and Iowa state income tax treatment was not researched; state rules on gambling-loss deductions can differ from federal.
- Interest and reward payments (Kalshi interest, promotional credits) are separately reportable ordinary income and are the items most likely to appear on a 1099.

### Gaps
- No primary IRS, Treasury or platform tax-center page was read; all tax findings are from secondary tax blogs and CPA marketing pages.
- No information found on what tax forms Polymarket US (QCX) issues for tax year 2026.
- Status of H.R. 10357 and any other pending fix to the 90% cap is unverified.
- State-level (Illinois, Iowa) tax treatment not researched.
- This is not tax advice; a professional should confirm classification before filing.

## 8. Minimum practical bankroll: order minimums, tick sizes, and when fees and spreads stop dominating

### Takeaway
Hard minimums are tiny (orders of a few dollars or less), so the binding constraint is cost, not minimum size: taker fees of roughly 3.5% to 4.75% of notional at mid prices, plus spread, make small taker-style strategies uneconomic, while maker orders are free on Polymarket US standard markets and most Kalshi series. Reliable, officially sourced minimum order and tick sizes for Polymarket US and Kalshi were not obtained, so a precise bankroll floor cannot be stated from sources.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Worked cost examples from the published formulas (taker, p = 0.50, 100 contracts, $50 notional): Kalshi 0.07 gives $1.75; Polymarket US entry tier 0.095 gives $2.375; Polymarket US top tier or first 7 days 0.0695 gives about $1.74. At p = 0.20 (100 contracts, $20 notional): Kalshi $1.12 (5.6% of notional), Polymarket US entry tier $1.52 (7.6%). At p = 0.90 ($90 notional): Kalshi $0.63 (0.7%), Polymarket US entry tier $0.855 (0.95%).
- The required edge per trade for a taker that holds to settlement at mid prices is therefore about 3.5 to 5 cents on the dollar of notional plus half the spread; for a taker that exits early, about double. This is true at any account size because the fee is proportional (apart from Kalshi's round-up, which penalises orders of 1 to a few contracts if cent rounding applies).
- Account size matters mainly through: (1) rounding and minimum-order effects on very small orders; (2) diversification across enough independent bets for the edge to show (dozens of positions); (3) the Kalshi $250 threshold for interest; (4) fixed funding frictions (ACH free; debit 2% on Kalshi). On that reasoning a few hundred dollars (about $250 to $500) is a sensible floor for a live experiment with $5 to $25 positions, and $1,000 to $2,000 allows 40+ concurrent small positions; below about $100 the result would be dominated by rounding, minimum sizes and noise. These thresholds are judgment, not sourced figures.
- A small bot should default to resting limit (maker) orders and to holding to settlement where possible, should model the fee explicitly per order using the venue's formula, and should avoid cheap long-shot contracts as a taker, where the fee is the largest fraction of the stake.
- None of the volume-based rebate programs are reachable at this scale, so headline "maker rebate" and "taker rebate" marketing should be ignored in the bankroll model.

### Gaps
- Official minimum order size and tick size for Polymarket US were not found.
- Official minimum order size, tick size and fractional-contract rules for Kalshi were not found. Unverified background knowledge: Kalshi minimum 1 contract with 1-cent ticks on most markets; Polymarket International commonly 5 shares minimum for limit orders and about $1 for marketable orders, with 0.01 ticks tightening to 0.001 near price extremes. These must be confirmed from the live docs or market metadata before use.
- Typical spreads and depth by category on Polymarket US and Kalshi were not measured; spread may exceed fees in thin markets, and this is the largest unknown in the bankroll estimate.
- No source gave an evidence-based "minimum viable account size"; the figures in Inferences are reasoned estimates.
