## 1. Key papers and benchmarks: setup, sample size, headline numbers, Claude-specific results

### Takeaway
Across every live benchmark, frontier LLMs moved from clearly worse than human experts (2024) to roughly level with superforecasters and with the Kalshi market price on proper-scoring metrics (mid/late 2026), but "level on Brier" has almost never translated into trading profit, and the market price itself remains the hardest baseline. Claude models are consistently among the better-calibrated frontier models (best ECE on KalshiBench, top of FutureSearch's BTF-3 and FutureSim), yet the latest Claude on Prophet Arena (Opus 5.5) sits just below the Kalshi market line, and the one real-money result I found for a Claude model is a loss (Opus 4.5, -25.9%).

### Cited Findings

**Halawi et al. 2024, "Approaching Human-Level Forecasting with Language Models" (arXiv 2402.18563, NeurIPS 2024) [BACKTEST with post-cutoff test questions]**
- Retrieval-augmented GPT-4-era system scored average Brier 0.179 vs crowd aggregate 0.149 over 914 test questions and all retrieval dates; accuracy 71.5% vs crowd 77.0% — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.18563); [LessWrong write-up](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/K2F9g2aQubd7kwEr3/approaching-human-level-forecasting-with-language-models-2)
- Selective settings: when the crowd is uncertain (crowd forecast 0.3-0.7) the system scored 0.238 vs crowd 0.240; on early retrieval dates only it was worse, 0.185 vs 0.161; under all three favourable conditions combined, 0.240 vs 0.247 — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18563)
- Weighted average of system and crowd reportedly lowered Brier from 0.149 to 0.146 **[unverified — figure from a secondary review]**; authors' own write-up states that averaging system and crowd beats either alone — [Liner review](https://liner.com/review/approaching-humanlevel-forecasting-with-language-models); [LessWrong write-up](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/K2F9g2aQubd7kwEr3/approaching-human-level-forecasting-with-language-models-2)
- Ablations: the system is significantly worse without the fine-tuned reasoning model, and worse again with neither retrieval nor fine-tuning — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18563)
- A commenter questioned whether the 0.179 headline matches the per-retrieval-date figure in the paper; unresolved — [LessWrong thread](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/K2F9g2aQubd7kwEr3/approaching-human-level-forecasting-with-language-models-2)

**Schoenegger et al., "Wisdom of the Silicon Crowd" (arXiv 2402.19379; Science Advances) [LIVE]**
- Ensemble of 12 LLMs vs 925 human forecasters in a three-month tournament on 31 binary questions: the LLM crowd beat a no-information benchmark and was statistically indistinguishable from the human crowd — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379)
- Acquiescence bias: mean model predictions were significantly above 50% despite a near-even split of resolutions — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19379v6)
- Study 2 (GPT-4 and Claude 2): showing the model the human median improved its accuracy by 17-28%, but this was still less accurate than simply averaging the human and machine forecasts — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379); [LSE repository](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/125626)

**ForecastBench (Karger et al., arXiv 2409.19839; FRI) [LIVE]**
- Launch result (Oct 2024): superforecasters Brier 0.093, general public 0.107, best AI model 0.111 — [FRI on X](https://x.com/Research_FRI/status/1841070675382902861)
- On the market-question subset of the original paper, the market price alone scores Brier 0.096, i.e. the best LLM in Karger et al. (2024) would have done better by simply outputting the market price it was shown — [AIA Forecaster technical report, arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678)
- Scoring changed in March 2026 from raw Brier to "Brier Index" = (1 - sqrt(Brier)) x 100; 100 = perfect, 50 = always predicting 50%, 0 = maximally wrong — [FRI on X](https://x.com/Research_FRI/status/2029225759152226538)
- March 2026: superforecasters at Brier Index 70.6; best LLMs (CassiAI "ensemble_2_crowdadj" and xAI Grok 4.20 Preview) at 67.9, a 2.7-point gap **[page attribution uncertain: reported in a search summary of FRI posts]** — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human); May 2026 secondary summaries give ~70.2 vs ~67.8-67.9 — [Parallect summary](https://parallect.ai/reports/ai-superforecasters-forecastbench-may-2026-895525)
- Official tournament board (date of snapshot unclear, 2026): external submissions hold #2 and #3, superforecasters still #1; the gap of 0.017 is described as about one year of LLM progress — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)
- 16 July 2026: FRI published "AI models have likely reached parity with superforecasters on ForecastBench"; AI lead of 0.2-0.4 Brier Index points over the superforecaster median of 68.8, not statistically significant; 17 submissions rank above superforecasters on the preliminary leaderboard; an AI system ranked above superforecasters on market questions for the first time — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity); [FRI on X](https://x.com/Research_FRI/status/2102411966103093386)
- A superforecaster rematch was scheduled for October 2026; I found no published result as of 18 Nov 2026 — [FRI on X](https://x.com/Research_FRI/status/2102411966103093386)
- Projected parity date: linear extrapolation gives November 2026 (95% CI January 2026 - November 2027); an older projection gave October 2026 (95% CI December 2025 - September 2027); Metaculus's synthesis quotes the CI as December 2025 - January 2028 **[conflict between sources on CI bounds]** — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-llm-forecasting-model-forecastbench-benchmark); [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43363/ai-forecasting-in-2026/)
- Trend in best-model score (Epoch-sourced): 61.5 in 2023, 63.2 in 2024 (Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Oct 2024 version, was the best model that year), 67 in 2026 (GPT-5.5 high) — [aiadoption.org](https://aiadoption.org/data/forecastbench-ai-forecasting/)
- Critique ("copier line"): on market questions, 204 of 273 leaderboard entries (75%) score at or below the score of copying the market price; 442 of 527 forecasters score worse than the market price they were shown; ForecastBench's own baseline models score 51.5 without the price (50 = always saying 50%), 68.1 with the price, and would score 70.93 by submitting the price unchanged — [EA Forum, "The Copier Line"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- Critique (Good Judgment): the superforecaster baseline is a frozen July 2024 snapshot of individuals working largely alone; the preliminary board measures the question type where AI has the largest structural advantage; the parity comparison is extrapolated, not head-to-head — [Good Judgment, "Not So Fast"](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/not-so-fast); [Good Judgment, "What ForecastBench Doesn't Measure (Yet)"](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/what-forecastbench-doesnt-measure)
- Claude-specific: no 2026 ForecastBench score for a current Claude model was retrievable; in the archived 2024-25 board the best Claude 3.5 Sonnet configuration ranked 83rd overall — [ForecastBench archived leaderboard](https://www.forecastbench.org/assets/iclr2025/leaderboards/leaderboard_overall.html)

**KalshiBench, "Do LLMs Know What They Don't Know?" (Lukas Nel, arXiv 2512.16030, 17 Dec 2025; single-author preprint) [post-cutoff questions]**
- 300 Kalshi questions resolving after model cutoffs; models: Claude Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, DeepSeek-V3.2, Qwen3-235B, Kimi-K2 — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16030v1)

**PolyBench (Cheng, Liu, Long; arXiv 2604.14199, Apr 2026) [PAPER-$ against historical order books]**
- 38,666 binary Polymarket markets with order-book and news data; seven LLMs; 36,165 predictions on timestamp-locked states, 6-12 Feb 2026; only two models earned positive returns (MiMo-V2-Flash 17.6% CWR, Gemini-3-Flash 6.2% CWR); the other five lost money despite high stated confidence — [arXiv 2604.14199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14199)
- Fills are simulated from best asks upward until the capital limit; slippage at larger sizes erodes returns — [arXiv 2604.14199](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14199)
- An outside reader disputes the denominator, arguing the experiment covers 5,097 prediction-ready markets, not 38,666 — [GitHub issue](https://github.com/microprediction/latentreasoning/issues/62)

### Inferences
- Trend over time, same-benchmark comparisons only: ForecastBench best model 61.5 (2023) -> 63.2 (2024) -> ~67-68 (H1 2026) -> nominal parity with a 2024 superforecaster snapshot (Jul 2026). Metaculus head-to-head gap -11.3 (Q3 2024) -> -20.0 (Q2 2025, harder questions/stronger Pros) -> -1.25, non-significant (Spring 2026) -> bots top the open Cup (Summer 2026). Prophet Arena: models below market on return (Oct 2025) -> 13 entries above the Kalshi line on projected Brier (2026). The direction is consistent across three independent live benchmarks.
- Claude trajectory: Claude 3.5 Sonnet best ForecastBench model of 2024; Claude Opus (4.x) the weakest frontier model in Lightning Rod's Jul-Aug 2025 Polymarket test (0.221 vs market 0.170); Opus 4.5 best-calibrated on KalshiBench (Dec 2025) but lost 25.9% trading real money (early 2026); Opus 4.6 -> 4.8 -> 5 improve monotonically on BTF (0.131 -> 0.130 -> 0.116); Opus 5.5 leads FutureSim Q3 2026 and is tied with the Kalshi market on Prophet Arena. Claude's relative strength is calibration, not profitability.
- "Parity with superforecasters" and "beats the market" are different bars. The market price beat superforecaster-parity systems wherever both were scored (AIA MarketLiquid; Lightning Rod; ForecastBench copier line).
- Absolute Brier numbers are not comparable across benchmarks: question selection drives them (market baseline 0.187 in the Prophet Arena paper, 0.170 in Lightning Rod's set, 0.111 in MarketLiquid, ~0.05 on the Prophet Arena live board). Only the within-benchmark gap to the market baseline is meaningful.

## 2. Do LLM forecasts add information beyond the market price? Combination, disagreement trading, real vs paper profit

### Takeaway
The best-supported positive result is that averaging an LLM forecast with the market/crowd price beats the market alone by a small margin (Halawi; AIA 0.106 vs 0.111), which implies the LLM carries some independent signal; the best-supported negative result is that LLMs trading on that signal lose money live in most tests (all six models on Kalshi, -22.6% average; five of seven on PolyBench; Opus 4.5 -25.9%). The only positive real-money or live claims come from the systems' own builders and cover one to five months.

### Cited Findings
- Averaging LLM + crowd beats either alone (Halawi), and LLM + market ensemble 0.106 vs market 0.111 (AIA Forecaster) — [LessWrong write-up of Halawi et al.](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/K2F9g2aQubd7kwEr3/approaching-human-level-forecasting-with-language-models-2); [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678)
- Simple averaging of human median and machine forecast beat letting the LLM update on the human median itself (Schoenegger et al.) — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379)
- When LLMs are shown the market price, most do worse than repeating it: ForecastBench baselines score 68.1 with the price vs 70.93 for the unchanged price; 442 of 527 forecasters score below the price they were shown — [EA Forum, "The Copier Line"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- Without the market price, the same baselines score 51.5, barely above an always-50% forecaster (50) on market questions — [EA Forum, "The Copier Line"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)
- The system only beats the crowd where the crowd is itself uncertain (0.3-0.7): 0.238 vs 0.240 — a 0.002 edge (Halawi) — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18563)
- Theory + live test (Prophet Arena team, arXiv 2607.06166, 7 Jul 2026): a forecaster that beats the market under a strictly proper scoring rule can profit using a specific "proper betting" rule if the market is liquid enough, and that rule is essentially the only one with the guarantee; on order-book exchanges informed forecasters often lose money; the forecast-price gap must cover the liquidity loss. A month-long live Kalshi deployment returned +80.33% with Sharpe 3.35 **[authors' own claim; one month; not independently checked]** — [arXiv 2607.06166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06166); [Prophet Arena research note](https://www.prophetarena.co/research/when-do-prophets-profit)
- Real money, independent of the builder: all six frontier models lost on Kalshi (avg -22.6%); on Polymarket they averaged -1.1%; the most selective trader (18 trades) had the best win rate (71.4%); the most active Claude run (886 trades) lost 25.9% — [arXiv 2604.07355](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1)
- FutureSearch Markets (trades only where its forecast disagrees most with the market): Polymarket paper portfolio -19.6% from 24 Apr to 11 Aug 2026; Kalshi +49.8% on paper but +5.9% on the real account (trading since 9 Jun 2026) — [FutureSearch Markets](https://markets.futuresearch.ai/)
- Paper-trading against real order books: five of seven LLMs lost over one week of Polymarket snapshots; order-book slippage at larger sizes erodes returns — [arXiv 2604.14199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14199)
- Prophet Arena paper: every model's average return was below 1 (below break-even) even where Brier matched the market; payoffs depend heavily on market-implied probabilities and have high variance — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638v2)
- Media synthesis (Mar-2026 era): most AI bots in trading contests were still losing money; retail traders picked the right outcome more often than bots yet still lost because they entered late at worse prices **[unverified — search summary attributing to Bloomberg; original not retrieved]** — [DEV Community, "AI Agents Are Trading Real Money Now — Here Is the Verified Record"](https://dev.to/dronpancholi/ai-agents-are-trading-real-money-now-here-is-the-verified-record-not-the-hype-4bbe)
- Kalshi fee structure and maker/taker asymmetry are material to any divergence strategy (Burgi, Deng, Whelan, "Makers and Takers") — [Whelan working paper](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)

### Inferences
- Decision (1): "Can an LLM forecaster plausibly beat Polymarket prices after costs?" On the evidence, not as a stand-alone forecaster trading every market. The independent real-money record is negative; the positive records are builder-reported, short, and (FutureSearch) show a large paper-to-real gap (+49.8% paper vs +5.9% real on the same venue). The defensible claim is narrower: an LLM forecast contains some signal orthogonal to the price, worth roughly 0.003-0.005 Brier when blended, and extracting it requires selectivity and execution discipline.
- The paper-vs-real gap on FutureSearch's Kalshi book (about 44 percentage points) is the single most useful calibration of how much simulated fills overstate returns; treat any paper P&L as an upper bound.
- Design implication: treat the market price as the prior and the LLM as an adjustment. A posterior of the form w * LLM + (1 - w) * price with small w (the copier-line and Schoenegger results argue for well under 0.5) is better supported than using the raw LLM probability. Trade only when the blended estimate differs from the executable price by more than spread + fees + a margin for the LLM's own calibration error (ECE of 0.06-0.12 for the best models means disagreements under roughly 10 points are inside the model's own noise).
- Fewer, larger-disagreement trades beat many small ones in the one study that shows both (18 trades, 71.4% win rate vs 886 trades, -25.9%) — suggestive only: two models, confounded with model identity.
- Polymarket appeared kinder than Kalshi to LLM traders in the same study (-1.1% vs -22.6%); possible reasons are fees, market mix (Kalshi cohort was a curated set), or luck. The authors do not resolve it.

### Gaps
- No study found that backtests or live-tests a threshold rule ("trade only if |LLM - price| > X") on Polymarket with an out-of-sample curve of edge vs X. This is the experiment a practitioner most needs and it does not appear to exist publicly.
- No source separates gross edge from the effect of Polymarket's 2026 fee schedule and spreads on LLM strategies specifically.
- The +80.33% Kalshi deployment: holding sizes, capital, and whether fees are included were not retrievable.

## 3. Known failure modes

### Takeaway
The documented failure modes are overconfidence (worsened, in one study, by higher reasoning effort), near-zero value-add when the price is hidden, gullibility toward retrieved text (one planted article flips over half of forecasts), recency/rumour anchoring, acquiescence toward "yes", and — for evaluation rather than forecasting — backtests that leak the future through model weights, search indices and question selection.

### Cited Findings
- Overconfidence: all five frontier models on KalshiBench were systematically overconfident; best ECE 0.120 (Claude Opus 4.5); GPT-5.2-XHigh was right 33.7% of the time when it said 90-100% — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16030); [alphaXiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2512.16030)
- Stated confidence does not track profit: five of seven models lost money "despite high stated confidence" — [arXiv 2604.14199](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14199)
- Under-extremity / hedging: Metaculus found bots' forecasts improve with Platt scaling (which pushes probabilities away from 50%), by 0.016 Brier on binary questions — evidence that tournament bots on average hedge too much — [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43363/ai-forecasting-in-2026/); AIA Forecaster likewise frames Platt scaling as "extremizing" — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678)
- Acquiescence bias: mean LLM prediction significantly above 50% with roughly balanced outcomes — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.19379v6)
- News-poisoning ("The Corroboration Illusion", arXiv 2609.22246, Sep 2026): one planted article per question flips 56% of a retrieval-augmented forecaster's forecasts, five flip 69-73% across three forecasters, ten flip 89%; models treat any on-topic evidence as strong evidence; genuine coverage also misled them; three defences tested, each with a cheap bypass; retrieval made small forecasters worse calibrated by adding an optimism bias proportional to the number of documents — [arXiv 2609.22246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22246)
- Misleading evidence is adopted openly: with one misleading article inserted per question, most models adopt and cite it; only 2.5% of 489 questions showed adoption without mention — [arXiv 2607.08046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08046)
- Recency bias, rumour anchoring and definition drift when news context is added; news helps on average but can flip a correct answer to incorrect (Karkar & Chopra, arXiv 2511.18394, Nov 2025) — [arXiv 2511.18394](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.18394)
- Adversarial formatting in trading pipelines: a finance LLM failed to analyse manipulated headlines correctly 99% of the time under a homoglyph attack and 67% under invisible HTML text — [arXiv 2601.13082](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13082v1)
- Slower than markets near resolution; inaccurate event recall; misreading data sources — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638)
- Weak on open-ended numeric targets: agents do not surpass analysts on earnings/revenue (33-37% win rates) — [ICLR 2026 FutureX](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/21315abf210cef71317e887e9cda78b3-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Forecasting skill is highly variable by domain and by how the question is asked — [arXiv 2511.18394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18394v1)
- **Why retrospective backtests are unreliable** (Paleka, Goel, Geiping, Tramer, "Pitfalls in Evaluating Language Model Forecasters", arXiv 2506.00723; ICLR 2026): training cutoffs are a guideline, not a guarantee — models know some post-cutoff events; search-engine date filters leak, and the retriever was itself trained on future data; "logical leakage", where backtest question sets written or selected with hindsight constrain the answer; questions built from later news skew outcomes — [arXiv 2506.00723](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.00723v1)
- Prompting a model to pretend its knowledge stops earlier does not work ("Simulated Ignorance Fails", Li et al., IJCAI 2026); recommendation: do not benchmark on events before the model's cutoff — [IJCAI 2026 proceedings](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/0650.pdf)
- Leakage flags raised by benchmark authors themselves: AIA Forecaster (1.65% of search results leaked) and FutureSearch BTF-3 (Opus 5's May 2026 cutoff overlaps the question window) — [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/a82q6yd8zKpYk56cF/ai-forecasting-in-2026-what-11-analyses-say); [FutureSearch Evals](https://evals.futuresearch.ai/)

### Inferences
- Two opposite miscalibrations coexist: closed-book or lightly scaffolded frontier models are overconfident (KalshiBench), while tournament bots that ensemble and hedge are under-extreme (Metaculus Platt result). A pipeline must measure its own direction of miscalibration on its own resolved forecasts; a fixed correction borrowed from a paper can be wrong-signed.
- The poisoning results matter more on Polymarket than on a benchmark: thin markets attract parties with a financial motive to publish misleading content, and an LLM that reads the open web is the cheapest participant to move. Source allow-listing and requiring independent corroboration from distinct publishers are indicated, though the paper says simple defences were bypassed.
- Any backtest of a Claude-based forecaster on markets that resolved before the model's cutoff (or within a few months after it) should be treated as contaminated. The only clean evidence is forward paper trading with timestamps.

### Gaps
- Run-to-run inconsistency (same question, different forecasts) and its size for current models: no quantitative source found, although the benefit of ensembling (section 4) implies it is material.
- A direct, quantified "hedging toward 50%" study for 2026 frontier models: not found; evidence is indirect (Platt/extremizing gains).
- Poor base-rate use: no direct measurement found beyond the weak positive effect of base-rate prompts (section 4).
- Political vs numeric/price question accuracy split with numbers: not found.

## 4. Techniques with measured benefit

### Takeaway
Measured gains rank roughly: a stronger base model > agentic multi-source retrieval > ensembling ~10 runs > post-hoc calibration fitted on your own history > prompt wording (near zero). Blending with the market price is the largest single improvement where a price exists. Reasoning effort helps up to "high" in one study and hurt calibration in another.

### Cited Findings
- **Base model**: in Metaculus Q2 2025, having a state-of-the-art model (o3) mattered more than scaffolding; the gap between best model and Pros (~16 Peer points) is about one GPT-4o -> GPT-5.1 step — [Metaculus Q2 2025](https://www.metaculus.com/aib/2025/q2/); [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43363/ai-forecasting-in-2026/)
- **Scaffolding**: in Fall 2025 the top five scaffolded bots beat their unscaffolded baselines by 5-11 Peer points per question; 34 of 39 surveyed bot makers used a frontier model — [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43363/ai-forecasting-in-2026/)
- **Retrieval**: agentic search cut ForecastBench Brier from 0.1230 (no search) to 0.11435; non-agentic (single-shot) search gave only marginal gains — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678)
- **Retrieval breadth**: number of research providers a bot used correlated with tournament score (r = 0.42); no single search provider had a significant edge — [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43363/ai-forecasting-in-2026/)
- **Retrieval + fine-tuning ablation** (Halawi): removing the fine-tuned reasoner hurts significantly; removing retrieval as well hurts further — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18563)
- **Sequential search / belief tracking / tools**: removing any one degrades Brier Index by ~3-5 points (arXiv 2604.18576, 2026) — [Emergent Mind summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.18576)
- **Ensembling runs**: going from 1 to 10 forecasts substantially lowers Brier, with diminishing returns beyond 10 — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678); ensembling over 10 runs improved raw Brier by ~6.57% — [arXiv 2611.17735](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17735); higher K consistently improves proper-scoring metrics when trials are combined in logit space with shrinkage — [Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2604.18576](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.18576)
- **Aggregation rule**: Platt scaling applied to the arithmetic mean of k forecasts gave the lowest Brier in AIA's tests; a median synthesis scored 0.1138 — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678)
- **Post-hoc calibration (Platt scaling / extremizing)**: +0.016 Brier improvement on binary and +0.005 on multiple-choice questions for Metaculus bots (Q1+Q2 analysis) — [Metaculus synthesis](https://www.metaculus.com/notebooks/43363/ai-forecasting-in-2026/); but a fixed-coefficient Platt step worsened Brier by ~1.68% in another paper — [arXiv 2611.17735](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17735) **[conflict: helps when fitted, hurt when fixed]**
- **Multi-model ensembles**: 12-model "silicon crowd" matched the human crowd — [arXiv 2402.19379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.19379); the top LLM entry on ForecastBench in March 2026 was an ensemble with crowd adjustment (CassiAI "ensemble_2_crowdadj") — [FRI Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/llms-are-closing-the-gap-on-human)
- **Blend with market/crowd**: 0.106 vs 0.111 (AIA + market vs market); system + crowd beats either alone (Halawi) — [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678); [LessWrong write-up](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/K2F9g2aQubd7kwEr3/approaching-human-level-forecasting-with-language-models-2)
- **Prompt engineering** (Schoenegger, Jones, Tetlock, Mellers; arXiv 2506.01578, Jun 2025): 38 prompts across Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Claude 3.5 Haiku, GPT-4o and Llama 3.1 405B; most prompts give negligible gains; base-rate references give slight benefit; Bayesian-reasoning prompts can reduce accuracy; after multiple-comparison correction no prompt significantly beat a minimal control — [arXiv 2506.01578](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01578)
- **Structured scaffold vs plain think-and-search** (AuditForecast, arXiv 2609.05905; GPT-5.4, 90 Polymarket questions): scaffold beat the baseline on Brier at low, medium and high reasoning effort, with the largest gap at low effort — [arXiv 2609.05905](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05905)
- **Reasoning effort**: on FutureSim with GPT-5.5 across five effort levels, higher effort consistently improved Brier skill score, plateauing after "high"; effort "none" had extremely poor Brier skill while accuracy was less affected (i.e. reasoning matters mainly for calibration) — [arXiv 2605.15188](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15188); but on KalshiBench GPT-5.2-XHigh was far worse calibrated (ECE 0.395) than other configurations at comparable accuracy — [arXiv 2512.16030](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16030) **[conflict]**
- **Fine-tuning on resolved questions**: RL fine-tuning closed ~65% of the Brier gap between a 32B base model and the market (0.253 -> 0.199; market 0.170) [VENDOR] — [Lightning Rod blog](https://blog.lightningrod.ai/p/foresight-32b-beats-frontier-llms-on-live-polymarket-predictions); RL-tuned gpt-oss-120b became competitive with frontier models and decorrelated from them; Brier reward trained more stably than log score [VENDOR] — [Thinking Machines guest post](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/training-llms-to-predict-world-events/)
- **Update frequency**: Mantic's Metaculus Cup result is partly attributed to scoring that rewards continuous updating — [explainx.ai coverage](https://explainx.ai/blog/ai-beats-forecasters-metaculus-cup-coverage-2026)
- **Bet sizing**: in one LLM betting study (Claude 3.5 Haiku) the variable that mattered most was whether the model chose its own bet size **[unverified — secondary article]** — [Level Up Coding article](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/seven-ai-models-traded-prediction-markets-for-a-week-five-lost-money-111f228c6e6c)

### Inferences
- Decision (3), pipeline that maximises calibration, in order of evidential support: (a) use the strongest available model; (b) agentic, multi-provider retrieval with several search rounds rather than one query; (c) run about 10 independent samples and average in probability or logit space; (d) fit a Platt/logistic recalibration on your own resolved forecasts and refit periodically — do not hard-code coefficients; (e) blend with the market price, with the weight also fitted on your own history; (f) keep the prompt minimal with a base-rate/reference-class step, and skip elaborate "Bayesian" prompt templates; (g) use high but not maximum reasoning effort until your own data says otherwise; (h) re-forecast on a schedule and on news, since staleness near resolution is where LLMs lose to markets.
- Decorrelation is the property to optimise for, not stand-alone Brier: a forecaster that is as accurate as the market but decorrelated from it improves the blend, whereas one that is accurate because it reads the price adds nothing.
- Evidence for question decomposition and adversarial "argue both sides" prompting specifically: none quantified in the sources found; the prompt-engineering null result suggests expecting little from prompt-level versions of these, as opposed to architectural versions (separate agents, separate searches).

### Gaps
- No quantified ablation found for question decomposition, explicit "argue both sides"/red-team steps, or trimmed-mean vs median vs mean beyond AIA's single comparison.
- No evidence on Claude extended thinking / effort levels and event-forecasting calibration specifically; the two reasoning-effort results are both on GPT models and disagree.
- No source quantifies the optimal blend weight between LLM and market price, or how it varies with liquidity and horizon.

## 5. Which question types and horizons favour LLMs relative to markets

### Takeaway
Direct evidence is thin and partly contradictory. LLMs lose most clearly near resolution on liquid, high-attention markets (slower information aggregation). The places they have matched or beaten a market are sports in one vendor test, uncertain mid-probability questions by a hair in Halawi, and a looser Polymarket selection versus a curated Kalshi set in Prediction Arena. Separately, market-calibration studies show long-horizon and political markets are systematically underconfident, which is a structural opening that does not depend on LLM skill.

### Cited Findings
- LLMs aggregate information more slowly than markets as resolution nears — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638)
- LLM was worse than the crowd on early retrieval dates (far from resolution): 0.185 vs 0.161; and marginally better only where the crowd was uncertain (0.3-0.7): 0.238 vs 0.240 — [arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.18563)
- By category on live Polymarket questions, the market beat every LLM in every category except sports, where the fine-tuned Foresight-32B led — [Lightning Rod blog](https://blog.lightningrod.ai/p/foresight-32b-beats-frontier-llms-on-live-polymarket-predictions)
- Prophet Arena's near-parity-with-market result comes from a set that is 76% sports and 7% politics — [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638v2)
- Same models, same period: -22.6% on a curated Kalshi set vs -1.1% on Polymarket with open-ended market discovery; authors suggest a structured, curated format may disadvantage models whose strength is finding opportunities — [arXiv 2604.07355](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1)
- ForecastBench: without seeing the price, baseline LLMs are near chance on market questions (51.5 vs 50); LLM performance relative to superforecasters is strongest on market questions precisely because the price is supplied — [EA Forum, "The Copier Line"](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually); [Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/what-forecastbench-doesnt-measure)
- Numeric/financial targets: agents below Wall Street analysts on earnings and revenue — [ICLR 2026 FutureX](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/21315abf210cef71317e887e9cda78b3-Abstract-Conference.html)
- Strong calibration in structured domains and notable weakness in volatile ones **[secondary summary]** — [Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2511.18394](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2511.18394)
- Market side: calibration slope rises from ~0.99 within an hour of resolution to ~1.32 beyond a month (prices too close to 50% at long horizons); political markets are the most underconfident (Polymarket mean slope 1.31, Kalshi 1.64); short-horizon weather markets run the other way (slopes 0.69-0.97 within 48 hours) — [arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.19520v1)
- Polymarket weather markets: longshots overpriced at distant horizons, favourites overpriced in the final 1-12 hours — [arXiv 2610.13593](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.13593v1)

### Inferences
- Decision (2), conditions: the least-bad conditions for an LLM forecaster are (i) more than a few days from resolution, so the LLM's slowness is not decisive; (ii) markets priced in the middle of the range, where the market's own Brier is worst and disagreement is cheapest to act on; (iii) questions where public text is informative and the answer is not a fast-moving number; (iv) lower-attention markets — though (iv) is an inference from the Kalshi-vs-Polymarket and "opportunity discovery" observations, not a measured result, and thin markets carry the widest spreads.
- Long-horizon political markets being underconfident (slope ~1.3) means the favourite is systematically underpriced there. An LLM does not need a forecasting edge to exploit that — a rule-based tilt toward the favourite would capture it — but capital lock-up over months is the cost, and the LLM's worst relative performance in Halawi was also at long horizons, so the LLM should not be the reason for the trade.
- Avoid: final hours before resolution on liquid markets; live sports in-play; price/number threshold markets; any market where the LLM cannot beat "repeat the price" in a hidden-price test.

### Gaps
- No study found that stratifies LLM-minus-market Brier by market liquidity/volume or by days-to-resolution on Polymarket. The "thin, low-attention markets" hypothesis is untested in the public literature I could reach.
- Category-level LLM-vs-market numbers (politics vs crypto vs sports vs economics) from Prophet Arena, PolyBench and Lightning Rod were not retrievable.
- Sports results conflict in spirit: a fine-tuned model led the market on sports (Lightning Rod), while Claude Opus 4.8 reportedly lost 18.1% betting World Cup matches [unverified].

## 6. Efficiency of Polymarket itself as the bar to beat

### Takeaway
Polymarket prices are well calibrated in aggregate — platform-wide Brier of about 0.04-0.06, tightening to about 0.025 a day out — with the documented departures being underconfidence at long horizons and in political markets, some mid-range mispricing, and a contested favourite-longshot bias that is clearer on Kalshi than on Polymarket. Most of this evidence is from preprints, dashboards and independent analyses rather than peer-reviewed work.

### Cited Findings
- Independent analysis of 7,661 resolved binary Polymarket markets: Brier 0.025 at 24 hours and 0.042 at 30 days before resolution (7-day snapshot also taken); calibration excellent at the extremes with systematic underpricing in the middle; author flags selection bias (Polymarket lists what will attract volume) — [LessWrong, "Empirical calibration of Polymarket"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Hruc6Gwo3vBZFGb6v/empirical-calibration-of-polymarket-analysis-of-7-661)
- Polymarket's accuracy dashboard (data by Alex McCullough, snapshots at 1 month, 1 week, 1 day, 12 hours, 4 hours): 90.1% accuracy at 1 month and 98.5% at 4 hours with overall Brier 0.0627 on an unofficial mirror; a news report gives average Brier 0.0565 and 95.2% accuracy at 4 hours **[conflict; different snapshots]** — [unofficial mirror](https://polymarket.206.189.21.72.sslip.io/accuracy); [Bitcoin.com News](https://news.bitcoin.com/crowd-wisdom-proves-potent-polymarkets-predictions-hit-95-accuracy/)
- Horizon and domain decomposition across Kalshi and Polymarket (Nam Anh Le, arXiv 2602.19520; 292M-353M trades depending on version): slope ~0.99 within an hour vs ~1.32 beyond a month; political slope 1.31 on Polymarket and 1.64 on Kalshi; Kalshi's political underconfidence grows with trade size (1.19 for single contracts, 1.74 for trades over 100), an effect not robust on Polymarket; Polymarket comparison covers only three of six domains — [arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)
- Favourite-longshot bias on Polymarket: studied by Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe (arXiv 2609.12878, Sep 2026; findings not retrievable); an independent analysis of 46,168 resolved Kalshi and Polymarket markets finds no favourite-longshot bias on Polymarket (mid-range prices slightly overconfident near resolution) and a clear, growing-toward-close bias on Kalshi; another finds a small bias at the cheap end on both **[conflict]** — [arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12878); [GitHub, prediction-market-calibration](https://github.com/hrishi-tailor/prediction-market-calibration); [GitHub, pm-calibration](https://github.com/PDiTO/pm-calibration)
- Kalshi shows a systematic favourite-longshot bias (Burgi, Deng, Whelan) — [Whelan working paper](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf)
- Polymarket outperforms polls and leads Kalshi in price discovery, though substantial cross-platform price discrepancies persist (Ng, Peng, Tao, Zhou) — [SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5331995)
- Brier improves in every domain (NBA, film awards, elections, economic indicators) as resolution approaches; NBA Brier is high because games sit at 30-70% — [Laidlaw Scholars analysis](https://laidlawscholars.network/posts/polymarket-calibration-analysis-across-4-domains)
- On LLM benchmark question sets the market's Brier is far higher than the platform-wide figure: 0.170 (251 live Polymarket questions), 0.187 (Prophet Arena paper, Kalshi), 0.111 (AIA MarketLiquid) — [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/LightningRodLabs/foresight-32B/blob/main/README.md); [arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.17638v2); [arXiv 2511.07678](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.07678)

### Inferences
- The platform-wide Brier (~0.05) is dominated by markets already priced near 0 or 1; the tradeable, uncertain subset that benchmarks sample has market Brier of ~0.11-0.19. An LLM's edge has to be judged against the latter, and the best LLMs trail it by 0.015-0.05 Brier when forecasting blind.
- The market's measurable inefficiencies (long-horizon and political underconfidence, mid-range underpricing) are structural and could be harvested with simple rules; they are a more reliable source of edge than LLM forecasting skill, and the two can be combined (LLM as a filter that vetoes the rule when news contradicts it).
- Metaculus vs Polymarket: no head-to-head accuracy comparison was retrieved in this session.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Polymarket calibration study with 1-month / 1-week / 1-day Brier by category was retrievable; the 1-week figure in particular is missing from the sources read.
- Whether the favourite-longshot or long-horizon biases survive Polymarket's 2026 fees and spreads: the one project that claims to test "whether it survives trading costs" could not be read.
- Polymarket vs Metaculus community prediction accuracy: not found.
