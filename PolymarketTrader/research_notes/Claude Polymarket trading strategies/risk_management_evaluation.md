## 1. Position sizing for binary contracts (Kelly, fractional Kelly, shrinkage to market, correlated bets, caps)

### Takeaway
For a binary share bought at all-in cost c with estimated probability q, full Kelly is f* = (q − c)/(1 − c) of bankroll, but the literature is unanimous that an estimated q must be bet at well below full Kelly; with realistic LLM estimation noise (5–10 probability points) against 3–8 cent edges, the Baker–McHale style shrinkage factor comes out at roughly 0.1–0.5, which justifies quarter Kelly applied to a market-shrunk probability (effective ≈ 1/8 Kelly) plus hard caps of about 2% of bankroll per market.

### Cited Findings
- Baker & McHale (2013, *Decision Analysis* 10(3):189–199): the Kelly criterion "ignores uncertainty in the probability of winning the bet and uses an estimated probability"; they show that "to improve out-of-sample performance the size of the bet should be shrunk in the presence of this parameter uncertainty", compare several estimates of the shrinkage factor, and report from simulation and tennis-betting data that shrunken Kelly beats raw Kelly; one approximation gives a "back of envelope" correction usable by bettors — [INFORMS](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/deca.2013.0271); [EconPapers abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ordeca:v:10:y:2013:i:3:p:189-199)
- A secondary write-up reproduces the shrinkage coefficient as α = (p − q)² / ((p − q)² + σ²), i.e. SR²/(SR² + 1) where SR is edge divided by the standard deviation of the probability estimate; it states that "uncertainty ⇒ go sub-Kelly" is robust across uncertainty models, and that the optimal fraction varies with uncertainty rather than being a constant fraction. (Forum post; the search tool flagged its rendering as partly garbled, so verify against the paper before hard-coding.) — [LessWrong, "Never Go Full Kelly"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/TNWnK9g2EeRnQA8Dg/never-go-full-kelly)
- Chu, Wu & Swartz ("Modified Kelly criteria", *J. Quant. Analysis in Sports*) formalise the same idea: bet k·s*(q) where s*(p) is the Kelly fraction, q is an estimator of p and 0 < k < 1 is a shrinkage factor — [SFU preprint](https://www.sfu.ca/~tswartz/papers/kelly.pdf)
- MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba ("Good and bad properties of the Kelly criterion", 2010/2011): good — maximising expected log wealth gives the fastest asymptotic growth and minimises expected time to large goals; bad — it can recommend very large bets in the short term and suffer heavy losses in a bad run; fractional Kelly (they treat half and quarter Kelly explicitly via a risk-aversion parameter) lowers that risk at the cost of lower expected final wealth — [Berkeley-hosted PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf); [eScholarship version](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5mr5k8qj/qt5mr5k8qj.pdf); [EconPapers chapter abstract](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:wsi:wschap:9789811285530_0015)
- The same paper's figure (attributed to MacLean & Ziemba 1999, blackjack with 2% edge) plots probability of doubling/quadrupling before halving against bet fraction; fragments visible in search show about 0.67 at full Kelly and 0.50 at twice Kelly (table not fully verified) — [Berkeley-hosted PDF](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~aldous/157/Papers/Good_Bad_Kelly.pdf)
- Simulation write-up on fractional Kelly: halving the stake keeps roughly three quarters of the growth rate (a worked binary example gives 76.1%), while greatly reducing downside; halving the stake does not mechanically halve compounded drawdown — [M. Downey, "Why fractional Kelly?"](https://matthewdowney.github.io/uncertainty-kelly-criterion-optimal-bet-size)
- Simultaneous bets: Whitrow (2007, *JRSS C* 56(5):607–623) treats optimisation of many simultaneous bets under log utility as a generalisation of Kelly staking and needs numerical (stochastic-gradient) methods; no analytical solution exists for five or more simultaneous bets — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssc/v56y2007i5p607-623.html)
- For independent simultaneous single bets, a 2026 preprint (C. Long, not peer reviewed) reports the optimal stakes differ from one-at-a-time Kelly stakes only by a cubic-order shrinkage (i.e. negligible at small stake sizes) — [arXiv 2603.26620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26620)
- For non-independent propositions, Kelly betting "becomes problematic"; a correct treatment needs the joint distribution over outcomes (forum post, not a formal source) — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/97tCruegbz8GXRFzi)
- Empirical prior that the market is hard to beat as a taker: on Kalshi (more than 300,000 contracts) takers lose on average and lose most on cheap contracts; makers do better; average returns of about −20% before fees are reported in one version and about −22% after fees in another — [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan, "Makers and Takers"](https://www.karlwhelan.com/Papers/Kalshi.pdf); [CESifo WP 12122](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp12122.pdf)
- Fee input to the formula (see section 2 for detail): official docs give taker fee = C × feeRate × p × (1 − p) and "Makers are never charged fees" — [Polymarket docs, fees](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)

### Inferences
- **Kelly formulas [own calc, standard derivation].** Let a YES share cost p and pay 1. Net odds b = (1 − p)/p, so f*_YES = (b·q − (1 − q))/b = **(q − p)/(1 − p)** (fraction of bankroll spent on YES). Buying NO at price p_NO (≈ 1 − p_YES-bid): f*_NO = **((1 − q) − p_NO)/(1 − p_NO)**; with p_NO = 1 − p this is (p − q)/p. With a taker fee φ = r·p·(1 − p) per share, replace price by all-in cost c = p_ask + φ: **f* = (q − c)/(1 − c)**, trade only if q > c. Shares to buy = f·Bankroll/c. Fee collection mechanics (deducted in shares vs collateral) could not be verified and change c slightly; read the fee from the live API.
- **Worked numbers [own calc].** q=0.55, ask 0.50, no fee: f* = 10.0%; with r=0.04 (c=0.51): 8.2% → quarter Kelly 2.0%, eighth Kelly 1.0%. q=0.60 at 0.50 (r=0.04): full 18.4%, quarter 4.6%, eighth 2.3%. q=0.30 at 0.22: full 9.5%, quarter 2.4%. q=0.90 at 0.85: full 31%, quarter 7.7% — Kelly gets aggressive on favourites, which is exactly where an absolute cap must bind. q=0.10 at 0.05 (r=0.05): full 5.0%, quarter 1.3%.
- **Why full Kelly is dangerous under estimation error [own calc, small-edge quadratic approximation].** Betting λ× true Kelly yields growth ∝ (2λ − λ²): half Kelly keeps 75%, quarter Kelly 43.75%, eighth Kelly 23.4% of maximal growth; λ = 2 gives zero growth and λ > 2 negative growth. If the model's edge is overstated by a factor 1/θ (true edge = θ × estimated edge), then betting λ × estimated Kelly equals betting (λ/θ) × true Kelly. So if the real edge is half the estimate, "full Kelly" is in fact 2× Kelly (zero long-run growth with maximal variance), and if the real edge is a quarter of the estimate, half Kelly is already 2× Kelly. Quarter Kelly stays growth-positive unless the edge is overstated more than 8×; eighth Kelly unless more than 16×. The asymmetry (under-betting costs little growth, over-betting destroys it) is the core argument for small fractions.
- **Drawdown probabilities [own calc, continuous-time approximation; valid only if the edge estimates are right].** P(bankroll ever falls to fraction x) = x^(2/λ − 1). Full Kelly: 50% chance of halving, 75% chance of a 25% drawdown. Half Kelly: 12.5% and 42%. Quarter Kelly: 0.8% and 13% (30% drawdown: 8%). Eighth Kelly: ~0.003% and 1.3% (30% drawdown: 0.5%; 20% drawdown: 3.5%). P(double before halving) = 67% / 89% / 99.2% for full / half / quarter (the 67% matches the MacLean–Ziemba figure). Design consequence: at an intended eighth-Kelly sizing, a 25% drawdown has roughly a 1% probability under the model's own assumptions, so it is strong evidence the assumed edge is wrong and is a principled hard-stop level.
- **Shrinkage factor numbers [own calc using α = e²/(e² + σ²)].** Edge 3¢: α = 0.50 / 0.27 / 0.12 / 0.08 for σ = 3 / 5 / 8 / 10 points. Edge 5¢: 0.74 / 0.50 / 0.28 / 0.20. Edge 8¢: 0.88 / 0.72 / 0.50 / 0.39. Edge 10¢: 0.92 / 0.80 / 0.61 / 0.50. An LLM's run-to-run and prompt-to-prompt spread on a single question is plausibly several points, so σ of 5–10 points is the honest planning range; for 3–8¢ edges that gives α ≈ 0.1–0.5.
- **Kelly with shrinkage toward the market [own derivation].** Treat the market price p as a prior and the model output q_m as a noisy signal: q_s = p + w·(q_m − p), w ∈ [0, 1]. Under independent errors, the precision-weighted value is w = σ_mkt²/(σ_mkt² + σ_model²), i.e. w < 0.5 whenever the model is noisier than the market. Plugging q_s into Kelly gives f = w·(q_m − p)/(1 − p): **shrinking the probability by w is algebraically the same as betting w × Kelly** (before fees; with fees it is stricter, because the shrunk edge must still clear costs). Recommended [opinion]: w = 0.3–0.5 initially, re-estimated from own resolved data by regressing outcomes on (p, q_m); then multiply by a further λ = 0.25. Effective fraction ≈ 0.08–0.125 of naive Kelly.
- **Recommended sizing rule and caps for a $200–$5,000 risk-averse account [opinion, justified by the numbers above].**
  - stake = min( λ·(q_s − c)/(1 − c)·B , per-market cap , remaining event/cluster/total headroom , depth cap from section 3 ), with λ = 0.25 and B = current bankroll marked conservatively (positions at min(cost, current bid)).
  - Per-market cap: 2% of bankroll (hard maximum 3%). Rationale: a 5¢ shrunk edge at p = 0.5 gives quarter-Kelly ≈ 2%; anything larger only arises from big claimed edges or favourites, which are the cases where model error is most costly.
  - Per-event cap (all markets of one event, e.g. several strikes/dates of the same question): 4–5%. Per-correlated-cluster cap (same underlying driver: one election, one central-bank path, one conflict, one crypto price, one company): 8–10%. Compute cluster exposure as the worst-case joint loss (sum of costs of all positions that lose together), not the net.
  - Total open cost basis: ≤ 40–50% of bankroll; cash reserve ≥ 50%. Maximum 15–25 concurrent positions.
  - Never average down automatically; a re-estimate after an adverse move must come from a fresh LLM run with fresh data, and the per-market cap still applies to the summed position.
  - Never use Kelly on a favourite to exceed the cap: buying at 0.85–0.95 has small upside per share and total loss on a mis-resolution.
  - The whole bankroll should be an amount whose total loss is acceptable, because platform/custody risk (section 4) is not diversifiable inside the account.
- Simultaneous independent bets at these stake sizes need no joint optimisation (Long 2026: corrections are third order); correlated bets do, and the practical substitute is the cluster cap plus treating correlated positions as one bet for sizing.

- Thorp's original fractional-Kelly discussion ("The Kelly Criterion in Blackjack, Sports Betting and the Stock Market") and the MacLean–Ziemba doubling/halving table were not retrieved in full; the drawdown formula above is my derivation, not a quotation.

## 2. Minimum edge thresholds (spread, fees, estimation noise, time value of locked capital)

### Takeaway
A defensible entry rule is: shrunk edge (q_s − all-in cost) ≥ about 3–5 cents in the 0.15–0.85 price band after half-spread, taker fee and a capital-lockup hurdle, which with w ≈ 0.5 means the raw model–market disagreement must be roughly 8–10 cents or more; trades in sub-10-cent longshots should be avoided because spreads there are 13–18% of price and takers historically lose most there.

### Cited Findings
- Official fee formula: fee = C × feeRate × p × (1 − p), C = shares, p = price; only takers pay; makers are never charged — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)

Conflicts: another source reports sports at 0.05 after a July 2026 change and crypto at 0.07; a Substack gives a variant with an exponent term, and the docs' client snippet returns an exponent field, so the simple formula may be incomplete — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout). The docs warn against hard-coding rates and point to a per-token fee-rate endpoint — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)
- Fees were extended to nearly all categories effective March 30, 2026; geopolitical/world-events markets stay fee-free — [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-to-earn-1m-daily-after-fee-structure-update-on-march-30-2026); [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees)

- Polymarket pays a 4.00% annualised "Holding Reward" on position value in certain designated long-dated markets only (list set by Polymarket and changes; one source reports a 3.35% rate at another time) — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/polymarket-learn/trading/holding-rewards)

### Inferences
- **Fee in cents per share [own calc from the formula].** r = 0.03: 0.27¢ at p=0.10/0.90, 0.56¢ at 0.25/0.75, 0.75¢ at 0.50. r = 0.04: 0.36 / 0.75 / 1.00¢. r = 0.05: 0.45 / 0.94 / 1.25¢. r = 0.072: 0.65 / 1.35 / 1.80¢. As a share of notional the fee is r·(1 − p): 2% at p = 0.5 for r = 0.04, but 3.6% at p = 0.10. A round trip as taker pays it twice; holding to resolution pays it once; resting (maker) orders pay zero.
- **Spread in cents [own reading of the Dubach figures as relative spreads].** 400 bps of a 0.50 mid is about 2¢ full spread (1¢ half-spread); 1,300–1,800 bps of a 0.05 mid is about 0.65–0.9¢, i.e. the 1¢ tick dominates and the proportional cost is enormous. The panel is stratified and includes illiquid markets; liquid headline markets are often 1¢ wide.
- **Capital-lockup hurdle [own calc: p × r × T/365, at p = 0.50].** At the 3.86% T-bill rate: 0.04¢ for 7 days, 0.16¢ for 30, 0.48¢ for 90, 0.95¢ for 180, 1.93¢ for a year. At a 10% required return (more appropriate given platform and resolution risk): 0.41¢ / 1.23¢ / 2.47¢ / 5.0¢ for 30 / 90 / 180 / 365 days. At 15%: 0.62¢ / 1.85¢ / 3.7¢ / 7.5¢. So lockup is second-order for markets resolving within 30 days and comparable to the whole edge beyond about six months. Holding rewards offset this only on the designated markets.
- **Threshold construction [opinion].** Required raw disagreement = (half-spread + fee + hurdle + safety margin)/w. Example, politics market at 0.50, 30 days: 1.0¢ half-spread + 1.0¢ fee + 0.4¢ hurdle = 2.4¢ of cost; adding a 2–3¢ noise margin gives a 4.5–5.5¢ required shrunk edge measured from mid, i.e. 9–11¢ of raw model–market gap at w = 0.5. Equivalent simple rule for code: trade only if q_s − c ≥ max(3¢, 0.5·σ_est) where c already contains the ask and the fee, and reject if annualised expected return on cost (q_s − c)/c × 365/T is below 15%.
- A relative check is also needed at the tails: require (q_s − c)/c ≥ 10% and exclude prices below 0.10 and above 0.90 for a conservative mandate, because (a) proportional costs explode, (b) favourite positions risk 85–95¢ to make 5–15¢ and are the ones most exposed to mis-resolution, and (c) the Kalshi evidence shows takers in longshots lose most.
- Posting passive limit orders (maker) removes the fee and earns rather than pays the half-spread, which can halve the required threshold, at the price of adverse selection and non-fills (section 3).
- A persistent large disagreement with a liquid market is more likely model error or missing information than edge; an upper sanity bound (e.g. refuse to trade and flag for human review when |q_m − p| > 20–25¢ in a market with tight spread and deep book) is as important as the lower bound [opinion].

- The November 2026 T-bill rate was not found (latest data point August 2026).
- No published practitioner source was found stating a specific cents threshold for Polymarket; the "several cents plus half-spread" rule is assembled from cost components, not quoted.

## 3. Execution on thin order books (limit vs market, slippage, partial fills, stale quotes, exit vs hold)

### Takeaway
Use only limit orders priced off the live book (never unbounded market orders), size against displayed depth at or better than the limit, cancel-and-requote instead of resting stale quotes through news, and default to holding to resolution unless the price has moved to the shrunk estimate or resolution risk appears.

### Cited Findings
- The CLOB has four order types: GTC and GTD rest on the book at a limit price; FOK and FAK execute immediately against resting liquidity (FOK all-or-nothing, FAK fills what is available and cancels the rest) — [Polymarket docs, order lifecycle](https://docs.polymarket.com/concepts/order-lifecycle)
- Post-only orders are rejected if they would match immediately and work only with GTC/GTD — [Polymarket docs, create order](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/orders/create)
- Each market has its own tick size (typical values 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, 0.0001) and orders not conforming are rejected — [Polymarket docs (order creation, CN mirror)](https://docs.polymarket.com/cn/trading/orders/create.md)
- Rate limits are generous relative to a slow bot (e.g. POST /order sustained 120,000 per 10 minutes) — [Polymarket changelog](https://docs.polymarket.com/changelog/predictions)
- Depth is not concentrated at the top of book: the share of top-10 depth at level 1 is close to the uniform benchmark of 0.10, so walking the book costs roughly one tick per level of equal size — [arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)
- Books thin as markets approach close (within-category slope 0.55 on log seconds-to-close, t = 3.85; another version reports 0.818) — [arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)
- Trade direction inferred from the public feed agrees with on-chain ground truth in only about 59% of buckets, and the estimated effective half-spread changes sign across windows on a large share of markets, so feed-based transaction-cost estimates are unreliable; self-counterparty wash share has a 1% median with a 22% upper tail — [arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)
- New markets: an IMDEA study of the first 24 hours of Polymarket markets measures quoted spread, effective half-spread and price impact (descriptive only) — [IMDEA Networks](https://dspace.networks.imdea.org/bitstream/handle/20.500.12761/2065/Polymarket_Initial_Liquidity-3.pdf?sequence=1)
- Informed flow around news is real: AP reported at least 50 wallets placing large YES bets hours before the April 2026 US–Iran ceasefire announcement — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2026/04/09/newly-made-polymarket-accounts-won-massively-on-us-iran-ceasefire-bets); a report describes 152 wallets allegedly making $8 million on US military markets — [SMU/Inkl copy](https://skbi.smu.edu.sg/sites/skbi.smu.edu.sg/files/%5Bcurrent-domain%3Amachine_name%5D/news_room/20260821_Inkl_Polymarket%20faces%20fresh%20insider%20trading%20scrutiny%20after%20152%20wallets%20allegedly%20made%20%248%20million%20betting%20on%20US%20military%20markets_%20report.pdf); Congress members raised insider-trading concerns after the January 2026 Venezuela markets — [Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2026/01/12/polymarket-kalshi-insider-trading-prediction-markets-cftc-torres-titus-venezuela/)
- The April 28, 2026 V2 cutover wiped all open V1 orders with about an hour of downtime (resting orders are not durable across platform events) — [BlockEden summary](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/05/13/polymarket-clob-v2-pusd-prediction-markets-infrastructure/)

### Inferences
- **Order style [opinion].** Marketable limit = FAK (or FOK for all-or-nothing) with limit price = min(best ask + 1 tick, q_s − required edge − fee). Never send an order without a limit derived from the estimate. For non-urgent entries, a post-only GTD order at or one tick inside the best bid with a short expiry (15–60 minutes) saves the fee and half-spread, but the fill is adversely selected: it fills when someone with newer information sells into it.
- **Slippage estimate [own method].** Before each order, pull the book and compute the volume-weighted average price for the intended size; require VWAP − best ask ≤ 1 tick and order size ≤ 10–25% of displayed size at or better than the limit, and also ≤ a small share of 24h volume (e.g. 1–2%). At $4–$100 order sizes this binds only in thin markets, which is itself the signal to skip them. Recompute edge using the VWAP plus fee, not the mid.
- **Partial fills.** Treat the filled part as the position (it was sized conservatively anyway); cancel the remainder and do not chase unless a fresh check still shows the edge at the new price. Log requested size, filled size, average price, fee and book snapshot for every order.
- **Stale-quote and pick-off risk.** A resting order is a free option given to faster, better-informed traders. Mitigations: short GTD expiries; cancel all resting orders before scheduled events (data releases, votes, court decisions, game starts) and when mid moves more than 2–3¢ from where the estimate was made; never rest orders across the bot's own downtime (cancel-all on shutdown and on start-up); make the estimate's timestamp part of the order decision and refuse to trade on an estimate older than a set age (e.g. 6–12 hours, shorter for fast markets).
- **A sudden price move toward or past the model's estimate is news, not opportunity.** If the mid moved more than about 5¢ since the data used by the LLM was fetched, re-run research before any order [opinion].
- **Exit versus hold [opinion].** Default hold to resolution (one fee, one spread). Exit early when (a) price ≥ q_s − exit cost (edge consumed; freeing capital beats waiting for the last cents), (b) a fresh estimate reverses the sign of the edge by more than the round-trip cost, (c) a resolution-risk flag appears (clarification posted, proposal disputed, rules ambiguity discovered), or (d) the position trades at 0.97–0.99 with days still to wait: the remaining 1–3¢ is small against lockup plus mis-resolution tail risk. Do not use price-based stop-losses on binary contracts: adverse moves without new information increase the edge, and thin books make stops expensive; use information-based exits and the portfolio-level circuit breakers in section 7 instead.
- Because depth decays toward close, do not plan on being able to exit in the final hours; size as if every position will be held to resolution.

### Gaps
- No verified numbers for typical displayed depth in dollars by category, or realised slippage for $5–$100 orders; these should be measured during the paper phase from recorded book snapshots.
- Minimum order size and heartbeat/cancel-on-disconnect semantics for the V2 CLOB were not confirmed from official docs.
- The Dubach paper is an unreviewed preprint and its v1/v2 numbers differ; cite v2.

## 4. Polymarket platform and structural risks, with mitigations

### Takeaway
The dominant non-forecasting risk in 2024–2026 was resolution risk on subjectively worded or timing-ambiguous markets settled by concentrated UMA token votes (repeatedly against the plain reading and almost never refunded), followed by front-end/third-party compromises (June and October 2026) and platform migrations/outages; nearly all the notable disputes would have been screened out by a rule that only admits markets with a single named objective resolution source, no subjective terms, and no event-versus-announcement timing ambiguity.

### Cited Findings
**Resolution mechanics**

### Inferences
- **Rule-reading checks that would have screened out the listed disputes [own analysis].**
  1. Subjective or definitional key term in the question ("suit", "invade", "ceasefire", "agree to a deal", "banned", "declassify", "audit") → reject. Catches Zelensky, Venezuela, Iran ceasefire, Ukraine deal, TikTok, UFO, Fort Knox.
  2. Resolution source is "a consensus of credible reporting" or unspecified, rather than one named primary source (official statistic, exchange print at a timestamp, league result, certified election result, a specific filing) → reject or cut size. Catches most of the above.
  3. Event-versus-confirmation timing: if the event can occur inside the window but be disclosed after it, and the rules do not say which governs → reject. Catches Strategy/Bitcoin exactly.
  4. Title and rules text disagree, or the rules add conditions the title lacks → trade only on the rules text, and reject if the LLM's two readings imply different outcomes. Catches Venezuela.
  5. Market already carries an "additional context"/clarification note, an earlier dispute, or sibling markets of the same template were disputed → reject.
  6. Do not enter after a proposal has been made or while a dispute is live; do not buy 95–99¢ "sure things" in markets failing checks 1–3 (several of these losses were holders of near-certain positions).
  7. Geopolitics/war and "mention"-style markets fail these checks most often; geopolitics is also the fee-free category, so the fee schedule should not be allowed to pull the bot toward it.
  Implementation: have the LLM fill a structured rules checklist (named source? objective threshold? timing clause? subjective terms list? clarifications present?) and let deterministic code veto on any failure.
- **Governance-attack / whale-vote risk.** Not controllable by a small trader; exposure is limited by the market filter above, the 2% per-market cap, and avoiding small-cap markets where a voter's position can exceed the cost of swinging the vote. Assume no refund (refunds occurred in the Barron Trump case but not in Ukraine, Iran or Strategy).
- **Cancellation / 50-50 resolution.** A position bought above 0.50 loses on a 50-50 split; this is another reason to cap favourites and to avoid markets whose resolution could become unknowable. No Polymarket 50-50 example was found this session.
- **Delayed resolution lockup.** Disputes add 4–6 days by design and contentious ones longer; the capital hurdle in section 2 should assume resolution date + 7 days, and no position should be needed for liquidity.
- **Smart-contract / collateral / depeg.** Collateral is now pUSD (wrapper on native USDC), which adds a Polymarket contract layer on top of Circle risk. Mitigation: keep on-platform only the experiment bankroll; treat a USDC price below about $0.985 or a pUSD redemption problem as a halt condition; accept this as a total-loss tail.
- **Exchange outage / migration.** Cancel-all on start and stop; never depend on resting orders surviving; subscribe to the changelog; pin client library versions and expect breaking migrations (V1→V2 broke all integrations in April 2026); halt on repeated API errors.
- **Front-end/third-party compromise.** Both 2026 thefts came through the web front end and third-party scripts. An API-only bot with a dedicated wallet avoids the browser path entirely; do not log into the web UI with the bot's wallet, and do not sign anything in a browser with it.
- **Regulatory / geo enforcement.** Run the geoblock check from the bot's own egress IP at start-up and refuse to trade if blocked; do not use VPNs to evade restrictions (accounts can be frozen or forced into close-only); confirm legality in the operator's jurisdiction before going live. Ongoing US litigation and the CFTC probe mean access rules can change abruptly; a close-only or fully blocked state is a liquidity risk.

## 5. Operational security for the trading bot

### Takeaway
The realistic threat to a small bot operator in 2025–2026 is not the exchange but the toolchain: at least half a dozen documented campaigns used fake "Polymarket bot" GitHub repos and typosquatted npm, PyPI and crates.io packages whose install scripts read `.env` files and exfiltrate the wallet private key, so the design should assume the development machine can be compromised and bound the loss with a dedicated low-balance wallet, pinned and hash-verified official dependencies, and no third-party bot code.

### Cited Findings
- December 2025: SlowMist's CISO relayed a warning that the GitHub project "polymarket-copy-trading-bot" contained malicious code hidden across multiple commits and dependencies; it scanned and read configuration files, extracted private keys and sent them to an attacker server; one report names the hidden dependency as excluder-mcp-package@1.0.4 — [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-copy-trading-bot-project-found-to-contain-malicious-code-stealing-private-keys); [Bitrue](https://www.bitrue.com/blog/polymarket-bot-github-malware)
- Around late February 2026: 20+ malicious repositories under a hijacked GitHub organisation (Dev Protocol) used typosquatted npm packages; warning issues were reportedly deleted — [GitHub issue (proxied copy)](https://proxy-ga.blitzz.co/proxy/123456/github.com/dev-protocol/protocol/issues/1334)
- OSV MAL-2026-4209: nine npm packages published by maintainer `polymarketdev` exfiltrate Ethereum private keys to a remote endpoint — [OSV](https://api.osv.dev/v1/vulns/MAL-2026-4209)
- `polymarket-clob@1.3.1` plus nine more npm packages masquerading as Polymarket CLOB libraries — [Safety](https://site.getsafety.com/blog-posts/polymarket-targeted-by-malicious-packages)
- July 1, 2026: SlowMist flagged the repo Trum3it/polymarket-arbitrage-bot; it used the real `@polymarket/clob-client` but also declared (never imported) a typosquat `clob-client-math` whose postinstall hook installed an infostealer; SafeDep tied 30 malicious npm packages across ten npm accounts to the one fake repo; at least 53 developers forked it; the stealer harvests wallet keys from `.env`, SSH keys, AWS logins, npm/PyPI tokens and password-manager data — [SafeDep](https://safedep.io/defi-infostealer-fake-arbitrage-bot-npm/); [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/defi-polymarket-users-targeted-npm-package/)
- OSV MAL-2026-14050: `@polymarkets/clob-client-v2` (note the plural scope), version 1.0.6, typosquat of the official V2 client — [OSV](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/MAL-2026-14050)
- npm `polymarket-copy-trading`: prompts for the wallet private key at install and posts it out, and also passively reads `.env` in the working directory to extract PRIVATE_KEY — [OffSeq Radar](https://radar.offseq.com/threat/malicious-code-in-polymarket-copy-trading-npm-cc7a3fc9b5085f12)
- A coordinated campaign of 25 crypto/web3 typosquat packages under one npm scope was published on 2026-07-09 (includes `@wagni_bot/polymarket-sdk`) — [OSV advisory (the mapping of this advisory ID to the package was not verified; search OSV by package name)](https://osv.dev/vulnerability/MAL-2026-10033)
- PyPI: `py-clob-clients` 0.1.8 (plural; typosquat of the official `py-clob-client`) exfiltrates environment variables from `.env` files — [GCVE mirror of MAL-2026-3220](https://db.gcve.eu/vuln/MAL-2026-3220)
- crates.io: `polymarkets-rs-clob-client` removed for malicious code; an ongoing campaign typosquats the polymarket-client-sdk ecosystem to steal credentials — [RustSec RUSTSEC-2026-0016](https://rustsec.org/advisories/RUSTSEC-2026-0016); [PT Security](https://dbugs.ptsecurity.com/vulnerability/PT-2026-22656)
- Allowances: EOA/MetaMask-style wallets must set token allowances themselves (proxy/Safe-type wallets do not); approvals are for the collateral token and the Conditional Tokens to the CTF Exchange, and additionally the neg-risk exchange and neg-risk adapter; community guides and SDKs routinely approve MAX_INT — [Polymarket market-maker setup docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/developers/market-makers/setup); [polymarket-client-sdk README](https://docs.rs/crate/polymarket-client-sdk/0.3.1/source/README.md); [NautilusTrader integration docs](https://nautilustrader.io/docs/nightly/integrations/polymarket/)

- OWASP guidance relevant to key handling in LLM apps: keep API tokens in application code rather than giving them to the model, and restrict the model's privileges to the minimum — [OWASP LLM01:2025](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)

### Inferences
- **Wallet design [opinion].** One dedicated hot wallet created solely for the experiment; fund it only with the bankroll (plus a little gas token if needed); never import the main wallet's seed on the bot machine; never reuse the key elsewhere. Sweep profits above the bankroll ceiling out manually. The maximum loss from any key theft is then the bankroll.
- **Private key versus API credentials.** Order placement requires signing with the wallet key, so the bot process needs the private key (or a signer); the CLOB API key/secret/passphrase are derived credentials for authenticated REST calls. Treat both as secrets; assume a leaked private key is total loss of wallet contents and a leaked API credential allows at least order manipulation. (Exact capability split not verified this session.)
- **Secrets hygiene.** The documented stealers specifically look for `PRIVATE_KEY` in `.env` in the working directory. So: do not store the key in a repo-local `.env`; load it from an OS keyring, an encrypted file outside the project tree, or a secrets manager; `.gitignore` plus a pre-commit secret scan; file mode 600; never print or log the key or signed payloads; separate OS user or container for the bot with no SSH keys, cloud credentials or browser profiles present.
- **Dependencies.** Install only the official client named in Polymarket's docs, by exact name (watch plural/suffix variants: `py-clob-clients`, `@polymarkets/...`, `clob-client-math`, `polymarket-clob`); pin exact versions with hashes (`pip install --require-hashes` / lockfile); for npm use `--ignore-scripts` and a lockfile; review diffs on every upgrade; keep the dependency list minimal; run an OSV/pip-audit check in CI. Never clone and run a third-party "Polymarket bot", copy-trader or arbitrage repo, including ones surfaced by search engines or AI assistants; if code must be inspected, do it in a throwaway VM with no keys.
- **Allowance scope.** With a dedicated wallet, an unlimited approval to the official exchange contracts exposes only that wallet's balance; still, verify contract addresses against official docs (the V2 migration changed them), prefer finite approvals sized to the bankroll if the client supports it, and revoke allowances when the experiment ends.
- **Separation of duties in code.** The module that holds the signer exposes one narrow function (place/cancel a limit order within pre-validated bounds); the LLM-facing research module runs without access to the key, the signer, or the network path to the CLOB.

### Gaps
- No write-ups from Socket, StepSecurity or Kaspersky specifically on Polymarket packages were found; coverage came from SlowMist (via news), SafeDep, Safety, OSV, RustSec and PT Security.
- Whether CLOB API credentials alone can move funds, and Polymarket's own official guidance on finite versus unlimited approvals, were not confirmed.
- Dates across reports of the SlowMist warnings conflict (December 21, 2025 versus July 1, 2026); these appear to be two separate incidents but that was not fully resolved.

## 6. LLM-specific risks and mitigations

### Takeaway
Prompt injection through fetched content is unsolved even for the best-defended agents (Anthropic reports about 1% attack success for a browser agent with all safeguards under adaptive attack, and published incidents show agents with wallet authority being drained), so the architecture must make a successful injection worthless: the LLM emits only a schema-validated probability and rationale, has no wallet or order tools, and every limit is enforced in deterministic code.

### Cited Findings
- OWASP LLM01:2025 states it is unclear whether fool-proof prevention of prompt injection exists; indirect injection arises when the model ingests external content such as web pages or files; recommended mitigations include least-privilege access, keeping API tokens in application code rather than with the model, and human-in-the-loop approval for privileged operations — [OWASP GenAI](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/)
- Anthropic (November 2025): for Claude Opus 4.5 in browser use under an internal adaptive best-of-N attacker, attack success was 8.0% for the model alone, 7.6% with earlier safeguards and 1.4% with improved safeguards; Anthropic describes roughly 1% as progress that "still represents meaningful risk" and says prompt injection is "far from a solved problem"; defences are layered (training on injected content, classifiers scanning untrusted content, red teaming) — [Anthropic, prompt injection defenses](https://www.anthropic.com/research/prompt-injection-defenses); [Anthropic, trustworthy agents](https://anthropic.com/research/trustworthy-agents)
- Independent benchmark: visual prompt injection succeeded against computer-use agents at high rates (Anthropic's agent below 60% across platforms, others often 100%), and a defensive system prompt had no significant effect (2025 paper, pre-dates current models) — [VPI-Bench, arXiv 2506.02456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02456)
- May 2026 incident: an X user used a Morse-code-encoded message to get Grok to authorise a $150,000 crypto transfer through the Bankr AI wallet; analysed as prompt injection via encoding plus excessive agency; about 80% was later returned — [Giskard](https://giskard.ai/knowledge/how-grok-got-prompt-injected-an-x-user-drained-150-000-from-an-ai-wallet); [NeuralTrust](https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/grok-morse-code)
- March 2025 incident: the AIXBT agent lost about 55.5 ETH after an attacker reached its control dashboard and queued fraudulent prompts (infrastructure compromise rather than classic injection; listed as alleged) — [AI Incident Database](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/1003)
- A multi-institution study reported that none of the tested AI trading-agent setups reliably defended against prompt injection; security firms advise that an agent that only analyses should not hold permission to execute trades — [KuCoin news](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/study-reveals-ai-trading-agents-vulnerable-to-prompt-injection-attacks)
- Knowledge-cutoff/lookahead problems are measurable: a "Lookahead Propensity" metric is materially positive in-sample and collapses to about zero after the training cutoff — [Gao, Jiang & Yan, arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847); in macro-forecasting "fake date" tests, none of the modern LLMs tested passed the first test — [arXiv 2601.07992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07992); claim-level analysis finds substantial temporal leakage in standard prompting, reduced 75–99% by a specialised method — [arXiv 2602.17234](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17234v1)
- Researchers "often assume models know nothing past their stated cutoff", which is unreliable; date-restricted retrieval also leaks (searching "January 6th" restricted to 2020 returns results skewed by later events) — [Paleka et al., "Pitfalls in Evaluating Language Model Forecasters", ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723)
- Prophet Arena (ICLR 2026) reports many LLMs have small calibration errors and promising market returns, with the advantage of strong models concentrated at extreme probabilities (0–0.1 and 0.9–1.0) — [alphaXiv 2510.17638](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2510.17638)

### Inferences
- **Attack surface specific to this bot.** Untrusted text enters via news/search results, social posts, market comments, and possibly market descriptions/clarification notes. An attacker with a position benefits from any text that shifts the model's probability ("official sources confirm...", fake press releases, SEO pages timed to a market). Unlike the Grok/Bankr case there is no transfer tool to hijack, so the realistic harm is a biased probability; that harm is bounded by shrinkage to market (w), the per-market cap and the edge sanity bound.
- **Architecture [opinion, consistent with OWASP least privilege].**
  1. LLM output is a strict JSON schema: market id, probability in [0.01, 0.99], confidence/uncertainty band, rules-checklist booleans, cited source URLs, short rationale. Anything failing validation = no trade. Free text is never parsed for instructions or sizes.
  2. The LLM never sees keys, balances beyond what is needed, or order tools; the sizing/execution module never reads LLM free text. No LLM-triggered code execution, shell, or arbitrary HTTP.
  3. Retrieval is allow-listed (major wires, official statistical/government sites, league sites, the market's own named resolution source); fetched content is wrapped and labelled as untrusted data; strip HTML/hidden text; cap document length.
  4. Deterministic vetoes: disagreement above 20–25¢ with a liquid market, estimate changed by more than 15 points between consecutive runs without a logged new source, rationale citing no allow-listed source, or rules checklist failure → no trade or human review.
  5. Self-consistency: run the estimate k = 3–5 times (and ideally with a paraphrased prompt and with the market price hidden versus shown); use the median, record the spread as σ_est for the shrinkage formula, and skip when the spread exceeds about 10 points. This also converts non-determinism from a risk into a noise measurement.
  6. Date grounding: inject today's date and the market's end date from code into every prompt; require the model to restate the current date and to list the publication dates of the evidence it relies on; reject evidence dated after "now" or with no date; never ask the model what today's date is.
  7. Hallucination control: every factual claim that drives the estimate must carry a URL from the fetched set; code verifies the URL was actually fetched in this run; optional second-pass verifier model checks claim-source support.
  8. Human approval tiers: fully automatic only below a small notional (e.g. ≤ 1% of bankroll and ≤ $10–25); human confirmation above that, for any new market category, and for the first N live trades; all automatic during paper trading.
  9. Append-only decision log for every cycle: prompt hash, model id and version, all fetched documents (hashed and stored), raw outputs of each sample, parsed probability, book snapshot, computed size, vetoes fired, order ids and fills. This is both the audit trail and the evaluation dataset.
- Model-version drift is a silent regime change: pin the model id, and treat a model change as a new strategy requiring a fresh paper-validation window.

### Gaps
- No documented case was found of a prediction-market bot specifically being manipulated through injected news content; the risk is inferred from adjacent incidents.
- A reported Claude Sonnet 4.6 system-card figure (8% one-shot attack success with safeguards, 50% with unbounded attempts) came from a secondary Substack and was not verified.
- No quantitative source on LLM run-to-run variance of probability forecasts was retrieved.

## 7. Circuit breakers and operational controls used by systematic traders

### Takeaway
Pre-trade limits and an automatic halt must live in code that runs before every order and cannot be overridden by the model; the canonical cautionary case (Knight Capital: 4 million erroneous orders in 45 minutes, $440M loss, no aggregate capital-threshold check) shows the failure mode is runaway order submission, not bad forecasts.

### Cited Findings
- Knight Capital, August 1, 2012: a router with discontinued code sent more than 4 million orders in the first 45 minutes of trading while trying to fill 212 customer orders; the loss was about $440 million — [SEC press release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222)
- The SEC found Knight's controls "were not capable of preventing the entry of orders that exceeded pre-set capital thresholds for the firm in the aggregate" and that it had not adequately reviewed the effectiveness of its controls; this was the first enforcement action under the Market Access Rule (15c3-5, adopted 2010), with a $12 million penalty — [SEC press release 2013-222](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2013-222); [WilmerHale summary](https://www.wilmerhale.com/insights/client-alerts/knight-capital-settles-rule-15c3-5-violations-with-sec-agrees-to-pay-12-million)
- Chicago Fed (October 2012): competition for speed pushed some firms to weaken pre-trade risk controls, and some relied solely on exchange-level checks instead of implementing their own — [Chicago Fed Letter No. 303](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/chicago-fed-letter/2012/october-303)
- Platform events a bot must tolerate: an hour-long cutover that wiped all open orders (April 28, 2026), a same-day trading halt (October 29, 2026), recurring maintenance halts (June 2026) — [BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/05/13/polymarket-clob-v2-pusd-prediction-markets-infrastructure/); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/polymarket-resumes-trading-vows-to-reimburse-traders-after-third-party-provider-compromise/); [mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/articles/polymarket-v2-6-1-triggers-outage-backlash-after-frequent-maintenance-halts-trading-in-june-2026)

### Inferences
Recommended control set [opinion; numbers scaled to an eighth-Kelly, 2%-per-market account, using the drawdown probabilities derived in section 1]:
- **Hard pre-trade checks (every order):** price within [0.10, 0.90] (or mandate band); limit price ≤ best ask + 1 tick and consistent with the estimate; order notional ≤ per-market cap and ≤ a fixed absolute dollar cap; resulting event, cluster and total exposure within caps; cash reserve preserved; market passes the rules filter and liquidity minimums; estimate age and book-snapshot age within limits (e.g. book ≤ 10 seconds, estimate ≤ 6–12 hours); no duplicate order for the same market within a cooldown (e.g. 1 hour); idempotent client order ids.
- **Rate limits:** max new positions per day (e.g. 5), max orders per day (e.g. 20 including cancels/replaces), max new notional per day (e.g. 10% of bankroll), max one order in flight at a time.
- **Loss limits (mark positions at current best bid, not mid):** daily loss ≥ 3–4% of bankroll → no new entries until next day; weekly loss ≥ 8% → pause and human review; drawdown from high-water mark ≥ 15% → halve all sizes and require review; ≥ 25% → full stop of the experiment pending post-mortem (about a 1% event if the assumed edge and eighth-Kelly sizing were right; about 13% at true quarter Kelly; so it is informative rather than noise).
- **Data sanity:** reject if best bid ≥ best ask, book empty or one-sided, spread > 5–6¢, mid moved > 5¢ since research data was fetched or > 10¢ in the last hour (news likely out; re-research), YES + NO asks inconsistent beyond fees, market flagged closed/paused/disputed, end date passed, API clock skew > a few seconds.
- **Reconciliation:** before each cycle compare local ledger with API-reported open orders, positions and collateral balance (and periodically on-chain balances); any mismatch above dust → cancel all, halt, alert. Reconcile fills to expected fees.
- **Kill switch:** a file/env flag checked before every order and a single command that cancels all open orders and stops the loop; cancel-all on start-up and shutdown; process watchdog so a crashed bot cannot leave orders resting; halt after N consecutive API errors or unexpected order rejections (e.g. 3).
- **Model/operations breakers:** halt on schema-validation failure rate above a threshold, on a model-id change, on LLM cost overrun, on geoblock check failure, on USDC/pUSD depeg signal, on dependency or config hash change without an explicit release step.
- **Deployment discipline (the Knight lesson):** one code path for paper and live with the venue adapter swapped; feature flags default to off; no dead code that can place orders; a dry-run at start-up; change log for every parameter change.
- Alerts (email/push) on every halt, every fill above a size, and a daily summary; a weekly manual review of the decision log.

### Gaps
- FIA's published best-practice guidance on automated-trading risk controls (order-size limits, price collars, message throttles, kill switches, drop-copy reconciliation) was not retrieved because the search budget ran out; the control list above is standard practice assembled from memory and the Knight findings, and the specific thresholds are recommendations, not sourced values.
- No public source was found describing the risk limits actually used by professional prediction-market trading firms.

## 8. Evaluation methodology (scoring rules, market baseline, calibration, CLV, power, paper-trading pitfalls, go-live and scaling criteria)

### Takeaway
At realistic edges of 3–5 cents, resolved-outcome P&L needs roughly 600–1,700 independent bets to show skill at 80% power, so the evaluation must lean on (a) paired proper-score comparison against the market price on the same questions and (b) closing-line value measured at fixed horizons, which can reach significance in roughly 50–300 trades; backtests are close to worthless for an LLM because of temporal leakage, so validation has to be forward (paper) trading with realistic fills.

### Cited Findings
- Sports-betting evidence that outcomes alone need very large samples: Buchdahl is reported as saying profit-based proof may take several thousand bets, whereas a consistent 5% edge over the closing line might need only about 50 bets to show significance (second-hand attribution; original not found) — [Pinnacle Odds Dropper](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value); a separate write-up of his analysis cites about 65 bets in a 952-bet sample — [Pinnacle Odds Dropper, CLV demystified](https://www.pinnacleoddsdropper.com/blog/closing-line-value--clv-demystified-by-expert-joseph-buchdahl)
- Buchdahl (interview): roughly 25% of bettors would be profitable after 1,000 bets and about 1 in 100 after 10,000 by chance alone; t-tests/z-tests and the closing line are the natural tools to test for skill — [Tradematesports interview](https://www.tradematesports.com/en/blog/interview-betting-expert-joseph-buchdahl/)
- The ratio of earlier prices to Pinnacle's closing price predicts expected returns almost one-to-one across 87,960 odds pairs (four seasons of European football), which is the empirical basis for CLV as a proxy for expected value — [How Pros Bet](https://howprosbet.com/what-is-closing-line-value/)
- Other practitioner sources put the minimum for meaningful CLV averages at 200–500 bets; a vendor study of 12,437 bets reports the CLV–profit correlation at 0.42 after 200 bets and 0.81 after 1,200 (unreviewed) — [Sharpery](https://sharpery.vercel.app/research/clv-long-term-profitability); Data Golf notes that below about 5,000 bets randomness still plays a meaningful role in results-based comparisons — [Data Golf](https://datagolf.com/analyzing-betting-odds)
- Evaluation pitfalls for LLM forecasters (Paleka, Goel, Geiping & Tramèr, ICLR 2026): temporal leakage through retrieval with unreliable date metadata and through retrievers trained on later data; over-reliance on stated model cutoffs; question selection at a past time can leak outcomes ("logical leakage"); question sets from forecasting platforms are skewed toward certain topics; calibration can penalise useful forecasting; Brier scores over-emphasise high-base-rate questions — [arXiv 2506.00723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00723); [ICLR 2026 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10006489)
- HINDCAST replays resolved Polymarket markets giving the model only content from before each market's cutoff, using a frozen snapshot, as a leakage-controlled design — [alphaXiv 2607.14051](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2607.14051.md)
- Lookahead bias is empirically detectable and collapses only after the training cutoff — [arXiv 2512.23847](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23847); none of the modern LLMs tested passed a "fake date" test in-sample — [arXiv 2601.07992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07992)
- The market baseline is strong: Brier 0.025 over 7,661 resolved Polymarket markets (heavily weighted to near-certain prices), with mid-range deviations and under-confidence in political markets reported elsewhere — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Hruc6Gwo3vBZFGb6v/empirical-calibration-of-polymarket-analysis-of-7-661); [alphaXiv 2602.19520](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)
- Microstructure facts that break naive fill assumptions: median spread about 400 bps mid-range and 1,300–1,800 bps below 0.10; depth spread across levels; depth decays toward close; feed-inferred trade direction only about 59% accurate — [arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)
- Proper scoring rules: the standard reference is Gneiting & Raftery (2007), "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation", JASA 102(477) (cited from background knowledge; not retrieved this session) — [JASA DOI](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)

### Inferences
- **Scores to compute on every forecast at decision time (whether or not traded) [standard definitions].** Brier = (q − y)²; log score = −[y·ln q + (1 − y)·ln(1 − q)] (clip q to [0.01, 0.99]). Both are strictly proper. Always compute the same score for the market mid at the same timestamp and report the paired difference per question with a bootstrap or t-based confidence interval, clustered by event. Report raw model q_m, shrunk q_s, and market p separately: the useful question is whether q_s beats p, and what w minimises the score.
- **Calibration.** Reliability table in deciles (and for the traded subset), plus the regression logit(y) on logit(p) and logit(q_m): the coefficient on the model term, given the market term, is the direct test of whether the model adds information beyond price, and its size is the empirical shrink weight w.
- **Power calculation for outcome P&L [own calc; one-sided α = 5%, power 80%; N = (1.645 + 0.842)² · q(1 − q)/e²; unit-share bets; simulation-checked: N = 618 → power 0.80 at e = 5¢, N = 1,717 → 0.80 at e = 3¢].**
  - Price 0.50: edge 2¢ → about 3,860 bets; 3¢ → 1,712; 5¢ → 612; 8¢ → 235; 10¢ → 148.
  - Price 0.30: 3¢ → 1,519; 5¢ → 563; 10¢ → 148.
  - Price 0.20: 3¢ → 1,217; 5¢ → 464; 10¢ → 130.
  - Price 0.10: 3¢ → 777; 5¢ → 315.
  - With only 50% power (i.e. "expected to just reach significance"): 3¢ → about 750; 5¢ → about 270 at price 0.50.
  - These are net-of-cost edges and independent bets. Correlated bets reduce effective N (ten markets on one event count as roughly one). At a pace of 3–5 independent resolved bets per week, 600 bets is two to four years: **realised P&L cannot validate a 3–5¢ edge within an experiment of a few months.**
- **Paired Brier difference is a weaker test than directional P&L [own calc].** If the model is exactly right with constant edge e, the per-question Brier improvement over the market has mean e² and SD 2e·√(q(1 − q)), giving z = e·√N / (2·√(q(1 − q))): half the z-score of the unit-bet P&L test, hence about 4× the sample (about 2,450 questions at 5¢, 6,850 at 3¢). A model can be directionally profitable while not yet beating the market on Brier. So use Brier/log score mainly as a harm detector (is the model significantly worse than the market?) and for calibration, and use market-relative directional tests for edge.
- **Closing-line value adapted to prediction markets [own method].** In binary markets the final price converges to 0 or 1, so "closing price" must be a pre-resolution reference: record the mid at fixed horizons after entry (+1h, +24h, +72h, +7d) and at a fixed point before resolution (e.g. last mid 24 hours before the end date or before the resolving event). CLV_i = direction × (p_later − p_entry-fill), in cents, with entry measured at the actual fill price including fee. Test mean CLV > 0 with standard errors clustered by event.
  - Power [own calc; N = 6.18·σ_Δ²/(c·e)², σ_Δ = SD of the price change over the horizon, c = fraction of the edge the later price incorporates]: with σ_Δ = 10¢ and e = 5¢: 25 trades if c = 1, 99 if c = 0.5, 396 if c = 0.25. With e = 3¢: 69 / 275 / 1,100. With σ_Δ = 5¢ (short horizons, quiet markets) and e = 5¢, c = 0.5: 25 trades. So CLV can separate a real 5¢ edge from zero in about 100–300 trades under plausible assumptions, roughly 3–10× faster than outcomes, consistent with the sports-betting claims (50–500 bets).
  - Caveats: CLV validates the model against the later market, not against truth, so it fails if the model is right in ways the market never prices before resolution, and it is biased if the bot's own orders move thin markets (use mids from books the bot did not touch, and horizons ≥ 1 hour). Positive CLV with negative realised P&L after several hundred bets points to costs or resolution losses rather than forecasting.
- **Paper-trading pitfalls and how to avoid them [own analysis grounded in the cited microstructure and leakage papers].**
  - Fills at mid: always simulate marketable orders against the recorded ask-side book (walk levels, add the fee); a 1–2¢ optimistic fill assumption is the same size as the edge being tested.
  - Ignoring depth and queue: cap simulated size by displayed depth; for passive orders count a fill only if the market subsequently traded through the limit price (not merely at it), and accept that passive fills are adversely selected.
  - Stale snapshots: decision and book snapshot must be from the same moment; log latency between research fetch and order decision and penalise by the price move in between.
  - Look-ahead: do not backtest an LLM on markets that resolved before (or near) its training cutoff, or with open web search; date filters on search engines leak. Only forward tests on markets unresolved at decision time count. If any historical replay is done, use a frozen pre-cutoff corpus design (HINDCAST-style) and treat it as a pipeline test, not as evidence of edge.
  - Survivorship/selection: log every market the screen considered, including those skipped, cancelled, or resolved ambiguously; define the market universe by a rule fixed in advance; do not evaluate only on markets that resolved cleanly or quickly (short-dated markets resolve first and bias early samples).
  - Correlation: count independent events, not markets; cluster standard errors.
  - No resolution-risk in paper results: apply a haircut or track disputed/clarified markets explicitly.
  - Parameter snooping: freeze thresholds, w, λ and the prompt before the validation window; any change restarts the clock.
  - Paper trading has no market impact and no behavioural pressure; expect live results to be worse.
- **Recommended phases and gates [opinion].**
  - Phase 0 (1–2 weeks): shadow mode, data collection, plumbing, reconciliation and kill-switch drills; no evaluation claims.
  - Phase 1 paper (minimum 8 weeks and until at least 150 forecasts on distinct events have resolved and at least 200 simulated entries have 72-hour CLV): gates to go live, all required: mean CLV at 24h/72h > 0 with clustered t ≥ 2 (or at least positive with t ≥ 1 and no contrary signal, if the sample is under 200); model-shrunk Brier and log score not significantly worse than the market's on the same questions, and coefficient on the model term in the logit regression positive; calibration error in the traded band within about 5 points per bin given sample noise; simulated net P&L after fees and book-walking ≥ 0; zero unexplained reconciliation breaks or limit breaches for 4 consecutive weeks; no rule-filter escapes (no held market later disputed for a reason the checklist should have caught).
  - Phase 2 live-small ($200–$500, per-position cap about $5–$10, human approval for the first 20–30 trades): run until 100+ live fills. Compare live fill prices with what the paper simulator would have assumed (slippage audit).
  - Scale-up (to at most $2,000–$5,000, in steps of no more than 2× per step): only after ≥ 200 live trades with clustered CLV t ≥ 2, realised net P&L ≥ 0 (or within one standard error of the CLV-implied expectation), drawdown never beyond 15%, and at least one full dispute-free resolution cycle; each step needs a further 100 trades.
  - Scale-down / shutdown: mean CLV < 0 with t ≤ −2 after 100+ trades; model Brier significantly worse than market after 150+ resolved events; 25% drawdown; any key-compromise or reconciliation incident; two rule-filter escapes; material platform event (unreimbursed hack, regulatory freeze, collateral problem).
  - Pre-register these gates in the repo before Phase 1 starts so they cannot be moved after seeing results.

## 9. What a deliberately risk-averse mandate would look like

### Takeaway
A conservative mandate restricts the universe to short-dated, liquid markets with a single objective resolution source, bets about 1–2% of bankroll per market at effective eighth Kelly with at least half the bankroll in cash, and treats capital preservation and learning (measured by CLV and calibration) as the objectives rather than return.

### Cited Findings
- Dispute risk clusters in subjectively worded, news-driven markets (suit, invasion, ceasefire, deal, ban, declassification) and in timing-ambiguous ones (Strategy), while Polymarket says only 0.2% of bets reach arbitration overall — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329210/polymarket-rules-no-237m-bet-zelenskyy-suit); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/polymarket-withholds-payouts-venezuela-invasion-130701655.html); [Galaxy Research](https://www.galaxy.com/insights/research/strategy-bitcoin-sale-polymarket-resolution-dispute-may-2026); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/polymarket-dispute-resolution-scrutiny/)
- Geopolitics is the fee-free category and also the one with repeated disputes and documented insider-timed flow — [Polymarket docs](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/prediction-markets/26/04/51712877/170m-us-iran-ceasefire-polymarket-takes-heat-over-insider-activity-resolution-process); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/business/2026/04/09/newly-made-polymarket-accounts-won-massively-on-us-iran-ceasefire-bets)
- Costs and adverse outcomes are worst in longshots: spreads of 1,300–1,800 bps below 0.10 and the largest taker losses on cheap contracts — [arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366); [Bürgi, Deng & Whelan](https://www.ifo.de/DocDL/cesifo1_wp12122.pdf)
- Depth thins toward close and books are not top-heavy — [arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)
- Shrinking stakes under parameter uncertainty improves out-of-sample results — [Baker & McHale](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/deca.2013.0271)
- Platform-level loss events occurred twice in 2026 through the web front end, with refunds promised — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/polymarket-hack-3-million-stolen-third-party-breach); [Unchained](https://unchainedcrypto.com/polymarket-resumes-trading-vows-to-reimburse-traders-after-third-party-provider-compromise/)

### Inferences
Suggested conservative mandate [opinion; every number is a starting default to be tuned from paper-phase data]:
- **Universe:** binary markets only (no neg-risk multi-outcome baskets at first); resolution by one named primary source with a numeric or categorical official outcome (economic data releases, central-bank decisions, scheduled votes with official tallies, sports results, exchange closing prints, certified election results); no markets with subjective terms, "consensus of credible reporting", clarification notes, prior disputes, or event-versus-announcement ambiguity; exclude geopolitics/war, "mention" markets, celebrity/culture gossip, and anything insider-prone (corporate actions, personnel decisions) unless the edge source is public data.
- **Liquidity:** 24h volume ≥ about $10k and total volume ≥ about $50k–$100k; spread ≤ 3¢; displayed depth within 2¢ of touch ≥ 10× the intended order; at least several days of trading history.
- **Price band:** 0.15–0.85 at entry (hard limits 0.10–0.90).
- **Time to resolution:** 2 to 45 days (hard maximum 60–90); nothing whose resolution could plausibly slip beyond the hurdle horizon; no entries inside the final 24 hours unless depth minimums still hold.
- **Sizing:** λ = 0.25 on a market-shrunk probability with w ≤ 0.5 (effective ≤ 1/8 Kelly); per-market 1–2% of bankroll; per-event 4–5%; per-cluster 8–10%; total deployed ≤ 40–50%; ≥ 50% cash; ≤ 15–25 positions; no leverage, no borrowing, no adding to losers without a fresh estimate.
- **Edge:** shrunk net edge ≥ 3–5¢ after ask, fee and hurdle; raw disagreement between 8¢ and 20–25¢ (above that → human review); annualised expected return on cost ≥ 15%.
- **Execution:** limit orders only; passive first where practical; order ≤ 10–25% of displayed depth at the limit; cancel-all around scheduled news and on shutdown.
- **Loss controls:** daily 3–4%, weekly 8%, drawdown 15% (halve) and 25% (stop); kill switch; reconciliation each cycle.
- **Custody:** dedicated wallet holding only the bankroll; API-only (no web UI with that wallet); profits swept out; bankroll sized as money that can be lost entirely.
- **Governance:** human approval above small notional and for the first live trades; pre-registered go-live, scale and shutdown gates; pinned model and dependencies; weekly review of the decision log.
- **Objective statement:** the first-year goal is evidence (positive CLV, calibration at least as good as the market on traded questions, zero operational incidents), not profit; expected dollar P&L at these sizes is small relative to noise (a 4¢ net edge on $10 positions is about $0.80 expected profit per trade at a 0.50 price), so LLM API cost per decision must be tracked against it and may exceed the trading edge at the low end of the bankroll range.
- Even "objective" categories carry model-specific traps: crypto price markets are the highest-fee category and dominated by fast bots; sports are efficiently priced against sportsbook lines; economic-data markets are where an LLM's edge is least obvious. The mandate reduces tail risk; it does not create edge.

### Gaps
- No empirical breakdown of dispute rates by market category or resolution-source type was found, so the claim that objective-source markets are materially safer rests on the pattern of notable cases, not on a base-rate table.
- Liquidity thresholds are judgment calls; no source gives recommended minimums for small accounts.
- Whether enough markets pass all filters to generate the 150–200 independent forecasts needed for the paper phase within about two to three months is unknown and should be measured in Phase 0.
